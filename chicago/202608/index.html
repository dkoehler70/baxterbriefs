August Edition — Thinner Field

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

377 vs 681
Distinct flip operators recording a resale this edition, against the same window a year ago

The flip field halved: 377 operators recorded an exit this edition against 681 a year ago, and the ones still selling are pricier and more concentrated

Flip exits fell to 534 from 922 a year ago, and the operator count behind them fell further, to 377 from 681. The ones still closing sold at a higher median price, $334,602 against $316,036, and held less time, 207 days versus 238.5. Concentration rose as the field thinned: the top ten operators took 18.8% of flip revenue, up from 11.2% a year ago. Cook County carried 65.9% of exits against 58.0%, and the median exit sat 27.1 km from the CBD versus 32.1 km. Recorded counts lag four to eight weeks.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer buyers on the list; price the spread to the ones still closing

Wholesalers
With 377 operators recording exits against 681 a year ago, the buyer bench is thinner and the median recorded spread has fallen to $44,000 from $53,750. The data suggests focusing on where flip demand still shows up in volume: 60628 (Roseland) logged 16 flips against 107 investor buys on a $132,000 median buy, and Roseland, 60620 and 60617 are the top areas for the 3-8 lifetime-flip operator band. Worth re-verifying buyer capacity before locking long inspection periods.

Exits are slower to appear than buys — watch what you add to a 15,507-unit pipeline

Flippers
938 flip acquisitions against 534 exits put 404 net units into inventory this edition, versus 321 a year ago; tracked pipeline stands at 15,507 units in the latest month. Median hold on what did sell was 207 days, down from 238.5, and median sale-to-assessed was 1.56 against 1.45. Worth checking whether new buys pencil at the current 1,400 sqft, 3-bed, 1956-vintage median profile at $237/sqft exit rather than at last year's assumptions.

The basis gap sits in 2-4 units and apartments, not single family

Landlords
Single-family buys cleared at $375,000 and $239/sqft this edition; 2-4 units at $277,320 and $137/sqft, apartments 5+ at $565,000 and $183/sqft. Condos, 4,119 deals, sat at $326,000 and $233/sqft — close to single-family per foot. Only 0.7% of flips were small multi, so hold buyers face limited competition from resale operators in that product. Worth checking Joliet ($281,000, $181/sqft) and Tinley Park ($320,000, $198/sqft) against the $415,000 Chicago median.

Recorded private paper is scarce; the rehab slot is where the volume sits

Private lenders
Only 33 private loans were recorded this edition against 93 a year ago, at a $267,000 median versus $161,250. The trailing-twelve-month file shows the gap being filled by scaled lenders: Kiavi 142 loans (86.6% rehab-funded, $231,200 median), RFLF 4 at 105 and Loan Funder at 102, all at loan-to-price above 1.2. Median financed loan across the market is $216,000 at 0.97 loan-to-price. Terms and rates are not recorded and are not published here.

897 apartment buys at the lowest per-foot basis in the file

Multifamily investors
Apartment 5+ was the third-largest residential product by count, 897 deals at a $565,000 median and $182.83/sqft — below single family and condo per foot. Twenty-nine apartment properties were resold as flips at $57/sqft, and three of the eight largest-margin exits this edition were Apartment 5+ in 60647, 60660 and 60657. Note that DuPage purchases read 767 against 1,725 a year ago, which may reflect a partial county file; worth confirming before drawing county-level conclusions.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Chicago-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
