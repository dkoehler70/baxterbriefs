August Edition — Flips Move Out

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

18.7 km vs 15.0 km a year ago
Median distance from Center City for homes sold as flips, current vs a year ago

Flip volume fell to a two-year low and the trade moved farther from Center City

Flips sold fell to 514 this period, down 37% from 812 a year ago, and the homes that did trade sat a median 18.7 km from Center City, up from 15.0 km. Median sale price held near $335,000, up 3% year over year, but gross margin narrowed to 75.6% of purchase price from 82.5%, suggesting acquisition costs are climbing faster than exit prices. The flip trade is smaller and more suburban than it was twelve months ago.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer assignments, bigger spreads

Wholesalers
Recorded assignments ticked up to 7 from 4 last period but remain half of last year's 14, with the median spread rising to $51,250 from $45,000. Worth checking Kingsessing (19143), where flip share of metro activity nearly tripled year over year and flip count (19) led every zip.

The core is thinning, the margin math is tightening

Flippers
Median flip distance from Center City rose to 18.7 km from 15.0 km, and gross margin narrowed to 75.6% of purchase price from 82.5%. Operators with 4-9 lifetime flips post a $196,720 median margin and a 122-day hold versus the market's 209.5 days — the data suggests concentrated, lower-basis buys in zips like Kensington ($75,000 median buy) or Frankford ($105,000) are worth checking against center-city acquisitions.

Hold-strategy formation is outpacing flip formation

Landlords
6,777 buyers added a combined 10,991 units since the last drop, and 13,164 first-time landlords entered the records. 2-4 unit purchases carried a $200,000 median ($125/sqft), well under single-family's $158/sqft — worth checking as flip volume cools and comparable stock may be repricing toward hold buyers.

Recorded volume has consolidated at the top

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 69 from 188 a year ago, with Kiavi, RFLF 4, Const Loan Services and Shallcross capturing most of the recorded volume. The data suggests room in smaller-balance, non-institutional lending outside those top names, particularly where seller financing (median $225,000) is filling gaps.

Larger product still trading despite the flip slowdown

Multifamily investors
Apartment 5+ deals cleared at a $1.6M median ($158/sqft), with this period's largest recorded transactions in West Philadelphia, Burlington and Newark. Worth checking whether cooling flip activity is loosening up 2-4 unit stock at a $200,000 median, a discount to single-family basis.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Philadelphia-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
