August Edition — Operator Thinning

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

339 vs 574
Distinct operators recording a flip sale this edition, against the same period a year ago

The flipper field thinned to 339 selling operators from 574 a year ago, and the ones still closing widened their median gross margin to $109,900

Flip exits fell to 629 from 996 a year ago, but the count of distinct operators recording a sale fell faster — 339 against 574. Those still closing took a median gross margin of $109,900, up from $100,000, at 37.5% of basis versus 34.0%. They also turned faster: 128 median days held against 147. Resale pricing did not do that work — the median flip sale is $410,000, flat year over year, and $254.61/sqft is below $268.83. Margins here are gross; rehab and carry costs are not in the public record.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer end buyers, wider spreads — requalify the list

Wholesalers
Only 339 operators recorded a flip sale this edition against 574 a year ago, so the buyer list is materially shorter while the median recorded assignment spread ran $36,628 against $19,500 a year ago. The data suggests concentrating on zips where exits are still clearing at working-class-free, deed-verified volume: 85208 Mesa Far East showed 20 flips against 74 investor buys at a $267,492 median buy, and 85351 showed 15 flips at a $235,000 median buy. Worth verifying which of your buyers actually recorded a sale in the last two periods before you tie up paper.

Margin is coming from basis, not from resale price

Flippers
Median gross margin is $109,900 and 37.5% of basis, up from $100,000 and 34.0%, while the median sale price of $410,000 is flat year over year and per-foot resale slipped to $254.61 from $268.83. Sale-to-assessed also eased to 1.28 from 1.33. The data suggests underwriting to a flat exit and taking the whole spread at purchase, with a 128-day median hold as the planning figure rather than last year's 147.

Buy-side is crowded but the median ticket is cheaper

Landlords
6,262 investor purchases recorded against 4,734 a year ago, yet the median purchase fell to $435,000 from $450,000. Worth checking mobile and manufactured product, where 250 deals cleared at a $171,750 median and $164/sqft, and 85208 Mesa Far East at a $267,492 median buy, against condo stock at $332,000 but $289/sqft. Per-foot basis, not headline price, is where the mix difference shows up.

Rehab money is the gap the record shows

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 85 from 260 a year ago while trailing-12-month data shows 72.3% of observed investor purchases financed, a median loan of $295,000, and a median loan-to-price of 0.96. Only 38.9% of the 2,606 financed purchases carried rehab funding, and among named lenders Kiavi ran 105 loans at a 71.4% rehab share versus Capital Fund I at 111 loans and 36.0%. With 206 homes aging out of the flip window in the latest month, refinance and extension paper on held inventory is the visible unmet demand. Rates and terms are modelled, not recorded, so price accordingly.

2-4 unit product is trading thin and rarely flipped

Multifamily investors
Only 76 two-to-four-unit purchases recorded this edition at a $940,000 median and $333/sqft, and just 4 flip exits were 2-4 units — 0.5% of flip volume. With comparable trades that scarce, the data suggests leaning on per-foot marks from the wider single-family set at $257/sqft and condo at $289/sqft when you build a value case, and expecting a long marketing period rather than a flipper-style turn.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
