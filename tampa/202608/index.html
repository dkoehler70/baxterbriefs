August Edition — Inventory Build

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

354 vs 170
Net homes added to flipper-held inventory this edition (820 bought less 466 resold), against 170 net additions in the same period a year ago

Flippers bought 820 and sold 466 this edition — net additions to flipper-held inventory doubled from a year ago

Flip acquisitions held up while exits did not. Operators bought 820 homes this edition versus 941 a year ago, but recorded only 466 resales against 771, a 40% drop. That left 354 homes net added to flipper-held inventory, against 170 a year ago. Deals that did close moved fast — a 141-day median hold versus 177 — so this reads as fewer exits reaching the closing table rather than distress pricing. Recorded counts lag four to eight weeks and the most recent month can grow up to about 15% as late instruments arrive.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Buyers are still buying; the constraint is exits, not appetite

Wholesalers
Flipper purchases ran 820 this edition against 941 a year ago, so acquisition demand held while resales fell 40%. Only 10 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds (a floor) versus 26 a year ago, at a $35,000 median spread versus $28,888. The lower-basis Pasco and Hernando zips carried the deal count worth working: 34668 Port Richey at a $165,000 median buy with 22 flips, 34667 Hudson at $160,000 with nine, and 34652 New Port Richey at $130,000 with seven.

Underwrite the queue ahead of you, not just the comp

Flippers
The pipeline held 12,635 flipper-owned homes in the latest month, with 261 aging out and 430 resold that month. With 354 homes net added this edition against 170 a year ago, the data suggests more competing exits in the same product later. The median exit was 1,404 sqft, three beds, built 1975, sold at $320,000 and $224/sqft; deals that cleared did so in 141 days. Worth checking your basis against the 45.1% median gross margin before adding another hold.

Two basis lanes are open below the $328,800 purchase median

Landlords
Investor purchases totalled 3,610 at a $328,800 median, roughly flat on $327,500 a year ago despite volume falling from 4,025. Condos ran 589 deals at a $195,000 median ($195/sqft) and mobile/manufactured 193 deals at $115,000 ($106/sqft), against single family at $301,000 ($209/sqft). Some 1,710 local landlords added units since the last drop, adding 2,694 units, so competition for the cheaper product is real; the data suggests pricing to the $153/sqft Spring Hill and $119/sqft New Port Richey city medians rather than to Tampa's $224.

Recorded private paper is thin where institutional rehab desks are not

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 45 from 176 a year ago, while rehab-funded loans made up 33.7% of the 2,158 financed purchases over the trailing twelve months. Kiavi (85 loans, 88% rehab, median $267,700) and RFLF 4 (75 loans, 92% rehab, median $308,232) dominate the mid-size rehab ticket. The gap the data suggests is the small balance: median flip exit is $320,000 with purchase medians of $130,000 to $195,000 in 34652, 34667, 34668 and 34608 — below the size these desks typically write. Rates and terms are not recorded and are not published here.

Small multi is a buy channel, not a flip channel, this period

Multifamily investors
Multi-family (unspecified) recorded 109 purchases at a $440,000 median ($232/sqft) and Apartment 5+ 16 purchases at a $1.7M median ($254/sqft), both above the single-family $209/sqft basis. On the exit side, small multi was 2.6% of flip sales versus 3.0% a year ago — thin either way. With flipper resale volume down 40% year over year, the data suggests holding units acquired at the $232/sqft level rather than underwriting a near-term resale into a market where 354 homes were added to flipper inventory this edition alone.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Tampa-St. Petersburg-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
