August Edition — Pipeline Build

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

402 bought vs 175 sold
Flipper purchases vs flip resales this edition; a year ago the same pair was 391 bought vs 266 sold

Flippers bought 402 homes this edition and sold 175 — acquisitions are outrunning exits by the widest margin in a year

Flipper acquisitions held up while exits thinned: 402 homes bought against 175 resold, versus 391 bought and 266 sold a year ago. Net additions to flipper-held inventory ran 227 this edition against 125 a year ago, and the standing pipeline sat at 4,509 homes in the latest month. Exits that did clear were not weak — median resale $1,025,000 against $990,000 a year ago, median gross margin $280,000 (36.4%) on a 126-day median hold. The gap is a timing problem, not a pricing one: more houses entering rehab than leaving it.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Spreads widened to $33,500 while flipper demand stayed intact

Wholesalers
Nineteen assignments surfaced on recorded deeds at a median $33,500 spread, against 9 last period and 2 a year ago at $22,500 — and that is a floor, since most assignments never touch a deed. With flippers still buying at 402 a period, the data suggests the bid is there for 1970s-1980s stock, which drew 212 and 196 investor purchases respectively. Worth checking 92117, 92126 and 92115, where flip resales concentrated this edition.

Check your own inventory before you add another house

Flippers
Net additions to flipper inventory ran 227 this edition versus 125 a year ago, with 4,509 homes standing in the latest month and 99 aging out. Exits that cleared did so in a median 126 days at a $280,000 gross margin (36.4%), so the earn is intact — the risk is holding depth. The data suggests underwriting to the 126-day median rather than the 30% that turned under 90 days, and treating anything past a year (10% of exits) as a carry problem. Rehab and carry are not public record, so these margins are gross.

Condo basis is $51/sqft under single family this edition

Landlords
Condos took 360 of the recorded investor purchases at a $665,000 median and $597/sqft, against 495 single family at $1,000,000 and $648/sqft. City Heights 92105 showed a $690,000 median buy and Mira Mesa 92126 $827,000 — the lowest medians among the busiest zips. With 1,351 local owners adding units since the last drop, worth checking whether the condo basis gap survives HOA carry in your target zips before assuming it is real.

Recorded private paper thinned to 37 while sellers carried 148

Private lenders
Private loans fell to 37 from 103 a year ago; over the same stretch seller notes rose to 148, median $340,000. Institutional rehab lenders dominate the trailing 12 months — ARV Finance recorded 223 loans (median $919,000, 81% rehab-funded) and Kiavi 62 (median $968,300). The median financed purchase carried a 0.95 loan-to-price and a $710,964 loan. The gap the data suggests is smaller, shorter paper behind seller carries, not competing at 95% on $900,000 purchases. Rates and terms are modelled, not recorded, so they are not published here.

Small multifamily is a buy-and-hold trade this edition, not a flip trade

Multifamily investors
Only 3.4% of flip resales were small multi, down from 6.0% a year ago, while investors recorded 46 2-4 unit purchases at a $1,200,000 median ($708/sqft) and 100 larger multi-family buys at $1,555,000 ($524/sqft). The per-square-foot spread between the two categories is the number to test in your own comps. Oceanside West 92054 (25 buys, $1.2M median) and Pacific Beach 92109 (42 buys) carried the most volume in the coastal band.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the San Diego-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
