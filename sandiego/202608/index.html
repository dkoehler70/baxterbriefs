August Edition — Faster Exits

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

126 vs 171.5 days
Median hold time on flips resold this edition, against the same period a year ago

Flips that cleared this edition sold in a median 126 days, 45 days faster than a year ago, at a wider gross margin

Only 177 flips resold this edition against 282 a year ago, but the exits that cleared were quicker and wider: a median 126 days held versus 171.5, and a $278,000 gross margin versus $250,000 (36.4% versus 34.6%). Median resale price ran $1,020,000 against $967,500, and $690/sqft against $670/sqft. Margins are gross — rehab and carry costs are not in public record. With 112 selling operators on file versus 182 a year ago, the volume drop reads as fewer participants clearing deals rather than softer exit pricing.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Buy-side demand is still there even as resales thin

Wholesalers
Flippers bought 314 homes in the latest recorded month against 146 resold, so the bid has not gone away. Recorded assignment spreads ran a $33,500 median this edition against $26,000 in the prior period. The data suggests working 1970s and 1980s stock — 212 and 196 investor purchases respectively — in 92117 and 92126, where flip resales concentrated. Late recordings can lift the newest month by roughly 15%.

Underwrite to a 126-day hold, not last year's 171

Flippers
The exits that cleared this edition held a median 126 days at a $278,000 gross margin, 36.4%, on a median 1,458 sqft, 3-bed, 1972-built house selling at $690/sqft. Worth checking basis in Clairemont (92117: $910,000 median buy, $1,270,000 median flip sale) and Mira Mesa (92126: $827,000 buy, $1,040,000 sale) against your own carry. Margins here are gross — rehab and carry are not recorded.

Condo basis is the cheaper entry per foot this edition

Landlords
Condos took 360 investor purchases at a $665,000 median and $597/sqft, against 495 single family at $1.0M and $648/sqft. Mobile and manufactured showed 6 deals at $366,900 and $142/sqft. With the overall investor median at $900,000 versus $927,500 in the prior period, the data suggests room to press on price in the condo band rather than chasing detached product.

The recorded private-loan gap sits under the flip pipeline

Private lenders
Only 37 private loans were recorded this edition against 103 a year ago, while 27.6% of the 1,598 financed purchases in the trailing twelve months carried rehab funds, at a $705,850 median loan and 0.925 median loan-to-price. Named rehab lenders dominate that share — ARV Finance 227 loans, Kiavi 63. The gap worth checking is short-hold takeout paper on the 126-day exit profile rather than long-dated positions.

Small multi is trading, but flippers have stepped back from it

Multifamily investors
Small multi made 3.4% of flip resales this edition versus 6.0% a year ago, so competition from renovation buyers on 2-4 unit product has thinned. Recorded purchases show 46 two-to-four unit deals at a $1.2M median and $708/sqft, against 100 unspecified multi-family at $1.555M and $524/sqft. The $/sqft gap between those two buckets is worth reconciling parcel by parcel before assuming a discount.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the San Diego-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
