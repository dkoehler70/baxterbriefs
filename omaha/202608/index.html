August Edition — Basis Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded February 1 – March 31, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$203,400 vs $155,000
Median investor purchase price this edition against a year ago, set against a flip resale median that fell to $249,500 from $345,000

Investors paid a median $203,400 this edition versus $155,000 a year ago, while flip resales fell to a median $249,500 from $345,000

Investor purchases ran 270 this edition against 215 a year ago, and the median price paid rose to $203,400 from $155,000. Exits moved the other way: 168 flips resold at a median $249,500, down from 213 at $345,000. Median gross margin on completed flips narrowed to 45.8% of the resale price from 67.3% a year ago, before rehab and carry, which are not in the record. The buy side is being priced as if exit values held. Counts lag four to eight weeks and the most recent month can still grow.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Volume is in 51501 and 68048; Elkhorn is an exit market, not a sourcing one

Wholesalers
Council Bluffs West (51501) took 31 investor buys at a $137,500 median and Plattsmouth (68048) 19 at a $52,000 median, the two deepest buy pools this edition. Elkhorn (68022) recorded 18 flips against only 6 buys at a $570,500 median, so the data suggests sourcing there means competing with retail. With the median investor purchase at $203,400 against $155,000 a year ago, worth re-testing whether your assignment spreads still survive the higher buy comps. Recorded wholesale counts are a floor.

Underwrite to 45.8% gross, not 67.3%, and to a 188-day hold

Flippers
Median gross margin on this edition's 168 resales was 45.8% of sale price against 67.3% a year ago, on a median hold of 188 days versus 179. Rehab and carry are not in the record, so that gross number is the ceiling, not the take. The mid-tier operator band (3-6 lifetime flips) shows a 142-day median hold on 1,203 sqft product built around 1962, concentrated in 51501 (27.9% of their work) and 51503 (13.2%). Worth checking whether smaller, older, closer-in product beats the $570,500-basis Elkhorn trade at today's $159/sqft exit.

Single family is priced at $147/sqft; the few 2-4 unit trades printed near $100

Landlords
Single family drove 242 of the period's purchases at a $203,400 median and $147/sqft. Recorded 2-4 unit trades were thin — 3 deals at a $105,000 median and $100/sqft — but that basis gap is worth checking parcel by parcel. North Omaha (68111) logged 15 buys at a $117,000 median and Council Bluffs East (51503) 16 at $138,750, the price bands where buy-and-hold basis still sits well under the metro median. 280 local landlords added 429 units since the last drop, so competition for those units is real.

Three recorded private loans against 21; the gap is being filled by institution paper

Private lenders
Recorded private lending fell to 3 loans at a $135,200 median from 21 at $185,000. Over the trailing twelve months, 342 of 548 observed investor purchases were financed, median loan $165,075 at a 1.0 median loan-to-price, and 47.7% of financed deals included rehab dollars. Dayspring Bank wrote 30 loans to 9 borrowers with a 70% rehab share; Strongco Holdings 17 loans at a $200,000 median. The data suggests the open slot is small-balance rehab paper under $165,075 on 1,200-1,500 sqft mid-century product, where seller notes (34 this edition at a $137,600 median) are currently doing the work.

Five-plus trades price at $227/sqft while flippers have vacated small multi entirely

Multifamily investors
Four apartment 5+ deals recorded at a $2.0M median and $227/sqft, and three multi-family (unspecified) at $448,000. Small multi was 0% of flip resales this edition, against 1.4% a year ago, so 2-4 unit stock is not being bid up by exit-driven buyers right now. With investor purchase medians at $203,400 and financed deals running a 1.0 median loan-to-price, worth checking whether 2-4 unit assemblages in 51501 and 51503 pencil against the $227/sqft being paid for larger assets.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Omaha-Council Bluffs-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated February 1 – March 31, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
