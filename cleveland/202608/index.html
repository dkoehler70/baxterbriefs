August Edition — Basis Reset

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$140,000 vs $152,000
Median investor purchase price this edition versus the same period a year ago, against flip resales at $185,000 vs $179,000

The buy side repriced and the exit did not: investors paid a median $140,000 this edition against $152,000 a year ago, while flips still resold at $185,000

Investors paid a median $140,000 this edition, down from $152,000 a year ago, while flips resold at $185,000 versus $179,000 — the acquisition side moved, the exit did not. Median gross margin per flip widened to $82,900 from $77,000, and median hold shortened to 191 days from 200. The qualifier is that margin as a share of basis slipped to 81% from 87%, and the resold stock is older: 60% pre-1950 versus 57%. Volume remains thin at 287 resales; pricing, not throughput, is carrying returns this period.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Spreads compressed with buyer basis; lowest-basis zips are still where the assignments clear

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds at a $19,575 median spread, against 20 at $36,250 a year ago — treat that as a floor, but the direction matches a buy side that repriced from $152,000 to $140,000. The data suggests the deepest cash demand is still in 44105 (111 buys, $45,000 median), 44108 (59 buys, $50,000) and 44112 ($30,200 median), where end-buyer expectations are set by basis, not retail comps. Worth rebuilding buyer lists around the 623 out-of-state purchasers, who transacted at a $152,500 median.

Underwrite the dollar margin, not the percentage — and to a 6-to-12-month carry

Flippers
Median gross margin per resale was $82,900 versus $77,000 a year ago, but margin as a share of basis fell to 81% from 87%, so the same dollars now sit on thinner relative cushion. Median hold ran 191 days with the middle half between 105 and 352 days and 24% of resales exceeding a year, which argues for carry assumptions well past two quarters. Zip pairs worth checking on spread between buy and resale medians: 44121 ($130,000 buy / $210,000 sale), 44135 ($122,500 / $210,000) and 44128 ($66,646 / $132,000).

The per-foot discount is in small multi, and 767 local owners are already taking it

Landlords
Multi-family traded at $51/sqft on a $103,000 median across 277 purchases, against $108/sqft for single family — the widest product-mix basis gap in this edition. Some 767 local landlords added 1,468 units since the last drop and 1,298 first-time local landlords appeared, so competition for the same stock is real. In Cleveland proper, the citywide $70/sqft median purchase compares with $87 in Euclid and $92 in Maple Heights, worth weighing against turnover and rehab depth rather than headline price.

Recorded private loans are near-absent; the gap is in the sub-$120,000 rehab ticket

Private lenders
Just 8 private loans were recorded at a $59,000 median, versus 56 a year ago, while seller-financed notes rose to 73. Program lenders now hold the rehab niche — Kiavi at 77 loans (1.30 loan-to-price, 84% rehab-funded), RFLF 4 at 61 (1.35, 95%) and MA6 at 38 on a $70,500 median price — leaving the smaller, older, one-off files thinly served. With median loan-to-price at 1.02 across all financed purchases, the data suggests pricing to a completed-value cushion rather than to purchase price on the pre-1950 stock that made up 60% of resales.

Apartment 5+ is clearing near small-multi economics per foot

Multifamily investors
Forty Apartment 5+ purchases recorded at a $340,500 median and $52/sqft, essentially the same per-foot basis as the 277 multi-family deals at $51/sqft. Small multi also grew as a share of flip resales, to 17.8% from 15.0%, so some of that supply is being renovated and re-listed rather than held. Recorded private financing for these assets is scarce this period, and the largest apartment loans on record ran through a single lender at a $1.6M median — worth planning capital stacks that do not assume a local private note.

trending down · resales 287 vs 619; net additions 201 vs 159
Flippers are buying about a third less than a year ago, but because resales fell further, the cohort is still net accumulating more homes than it did last year. That is inventory building on balance sheets, not clearing.

trending up · vs 55 a year ago, while recorded private loans ran 8 vs 56
Seller paper is expanding as recorded third-party private lending has all but disappeared from the deed record. For buyers of low-basis stock, the seller is increasingly the counterparty of last resort.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
