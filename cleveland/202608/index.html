August Edition — Basis Reset

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$140,000 vs $152,000
Median investor purchase price this edition against the same period a year ago, on essentially flat volume — 2,022 purchases vs 2,033

Investors bought the same number of homes for less: median purchase price fell to $140,000 from $152,000 a year ago on 2,022 deals vs 2,033

Investor purchase volume held near last year's level — 2,022 deals against 2,033 — but the price paid fell to a $140,000 median from $152,000. The reset is year-over-year rather than a straight slide: the prior period's median was $133,710. Exit pricing has not followed basis down. Flips resold at a $186,250 median against $179,950 a year ago, and the median flip margin widened to $82,950 from $76,750. Cleveland proper, 1,040 of the purchases, cleared at a $100,000 median and $70/sqft.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer assignments, thinner spreads — sell into the low-basis zips

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds at a $19,575 median spread, against 20 a year ago at $36,250; treat that as a floor. Buyer depth is still concentrated where basis is cheapest — 44105 took 111 investor buys at a $45,000 median, 44108 59 at $50,000, 44128 62 at $66,647. With buyers now paying a $140,000 median county-wide against $152,000 a year ago, it is worth re-pricing contracts to that lower comp set rather than last year's.

Exits are scarce but priced better — underwrite a 6-to-12-month hold

Flippers
288 flips sold this edition against 624 a year ago, yet the median resale was $186,250 versus $179,950 and the median margin $82,950 versus $76,750. The data suggests the constraint is throughput, not price: median hold ran 192.5 days with 25% over a year. Buy-to-sale gaps look widest in 44121 ($130,000 median buy vs $210,000 median sale) and 44135 ($122,500 vs $210,000); check carry to twelve months before committing, since 9% of the 6,715 flippers on record are net-negative lifetime.

The per-foot discount is in small multi, not in houses

Landlords
Multi-family traded at $51/sqft on a $103,000 median across 277 purchases while single family ran $108/sqft. Cleveland as a whole cleared $100,000 and $70/sqft on 1,040 buys, against Mentor at $205/sqft and Willoughby at $217/sqft. 767 local landlords added 1,468 units since the last drop and 1,298 first-time landlords appeared, so the competition at the cheap end is real — worth pricing rehab reserves off the $18/sqft flip resale basis seen in multi-family exits rather than off single-family comps.

Small multi under $100,000 is the underserved slot

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 8 at a $59,000 median from 56 a year ago, while Kiavi (1.30 loan-to-price, 84% rehab) and RFLF 4 (1.35, 95% rehab) dominate the funded rehab book and CrossCountry sits at 0.95 on a $230,000 median price. The private loans that did record were mostly 2-4 unit Cleveland assets at $93,000 to $100,000 — a band the program lenders largely skip. Seller notes at 73 and a $125,000 median show borrowers finding paper elsewhere when nobody funds that slot.

Small multi is a growing share of exits at a $50,000 median

Multifamily investors
Small multi made 17.7% of flip exits this edition against 14.9% a year ago, with 33 multi-family flips selling at a $50,000 median and $18/sqft. On the buy side, Apartment 5+ ran 40 deals at a $340,500 median and $52/sqft. The gap between a $103,000 median acquisition and a $50,000 median flip exit says renovated small multi is not clearing at a premium — the data suggests holding for rent, not resale, is where the current basis works.

trending down · vs $108/sqft for single family this edition
277 multi-family purchases cleared at a $103,000 median, about half the per-foot basis of the 1,266 single-family buys. Apartment 5+ came in at $52/sqft on 40 deals, confirming the discount is structural to the product, not to one price band.

trending down · vs 202 days on flips sold a year ago
The middle half of exits took 105 to 358 days, 22% turned in under 90 days and 25% sat more than a year. Hold assumptions of six months are the median case, not the safe case.

8 recorded private loans at a $59,000 median — Private mortgage instruments recorded this edition

trending down · vs 56 a year ago at a $100,000 median
Individual private lending has nearly vanished from the record while program lenders carry the rehab book: Kiavi funded 77 loans at a $150,500 median and 1.30 loan-to-price over the trailing year, RFLF 4 another 61 at $166,000 with a 95% rehab share. Recorded counts lag four to eight weeks.

73 seller-financed notes at a $125,000 median — New seller-held notes this edition

trending up · vs 55 a year ago at a $115,000 median
Seller paper is the one financing channel growing, up from 67 in the prior period as well. Where bank and private debt is scarce, sellers are carrying the gap, and 41 of the 73 were recorded in June alone.

44120 took 6.3% of flip exits — Shaker Square / Buckeye share of flips sold

trending up · vs 2.7% a year ago
The zip logged 18 flips and 75 investor buys at a $67,500 median buy against a $122,500 median sale. Maple Heights (44137) went the other way, from 2.9% of flips a year ago to none this edition.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
