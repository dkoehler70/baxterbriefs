August Edition — Exit Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

225 vs 388
Flips sold this edition against the same two months a year ago, while investor purchases ran 2,186 vs 2,255

Flips sold fell to 225 from 388 a year ago while investor purchases held near flat — the exit channel is narrowing faster than the buy side

Investors kept buying — 2,186 purchases against 2,255 a year ago — but far fewer of them closed a resale. Flips recorded this edition total 225, down from 296 in the prior period and 388 a year ago, and the median flip price fell to $62,500 from $74,650. The monthly detail shows the drift continuing at 127 flips in May and 98 in June, though the most recent month can grow up to about 15% as late records arrive. The offset is showing up on the hold side: 767 landlords added 1,468 units since the last drop.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Spreads are thinner and buyers are holding, not flipping

Wholesalers
Seven assignments surfaced on recorded deeds at a $19,575 median spread, against 20 a year ago at $36,250 — and recorded counts are a floor. With flip exits at 225 vs 388, the data suggests the reliable end buyer this period is a landlord, not a rehab-and-resale shop. Worth checking the $45,000–$67,546 median-buy zips — 44105, 44108, 44128 — where hold buyers are most active, and pricing assignments to a rent underwrite rather than a resale comp.

Underwrite the exit at $62,500, not last year's $74,650

Flippers
Median flip resale came in at $62,500, down 16% year over year, and single-family flips cleared 170 units at $68,822, or $50/sqft — against a $107.69/sqft single-family acquisition basis. Hold time in the latest measurable period ran a median 5.2 months, with 12% over a year. The data suggests building carry for two quarters and testing whether the deal still works if the exit prints in the $50k–$70k band that dominated this edition.

The 2–4 unit basis is where the volume went

Landlords
Investors took 286 multi-family parcels at a $103,000 median and $52/sqft, versus $107.69/sqft on single family, while 767 landlords added 1,468 units. Cleveland's citywide $105,000 median at $68/sqft and Euclid's $115,000 at $86/sqft remain the accessible tiers; Lakewood at $243,100 and Willoughby at $232,500 do not pencil the same way. Worth checking condo stock too — 262 traded at $189,900 and $166.93/sqft, the highest residential per-foot basis in the set.

Sellers are filling the gap you left

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 11 from 64 a year ago at a $60,000 median, while seller-financed notes rose to 91 from 72 at a $138,000 median. That is where the borrower demand went. With flip holds running a median 5.2 months and 22% under 90 days, the data suggests room for short bridge paper in the $60,000–$100,000 range on Cleveland 2–4 units — the size band the recorded loans this period actually occupied.

Small retail and 5+ units still clear near $50–$78 per foot

Commercial investors
Retail/service traded 78 times at a $314,750 median and $77.61/sqft; Apartment 5+ took 40 deals at $340,500 and $51.96/sqft; industrial 31 at $375,000 and $50.07/sqft; office 32 at a $538,750 median and $96.48/sqft. Only five retail flips resold, at $90,000 and $18.08/sqft, so the resale channel here is effectively closed. The data suggests underwriting these on hold economics, with attention to the per-foot spread between office and apartment stock.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
