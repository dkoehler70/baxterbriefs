August Edition — Inward Shift

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$75,000 vs $62,000
Median gross margin per flip sold this edition, against the same window a year ago

The flip field thinned and moved inward: 333 sellers took a $75,000 median margin against $62,000 for 775 a year ago

The flip field thinned, but the deals that closed paid better. Median margin per flip sold was $75,000, or 74% over basis, against $62,000 and 63% a year ago, while the count of distinct flippers selling fell to 333 from 775 and the top-10 share of exits dropped to 25% from 48%. The work moved inward and downmarket: Cuyahoga took 73.3% of flip exits versus 69.9%, Slavic Village/Union-Miles rose to 7.2% of flips from 4.7%, and North Royalton fell to 0.2% from 3.0%. Median hold shortened to 188 days from 236.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Rebuild the buyer list around 333 active exit-sellers, not the old top ten

Wholesalers
The top-10 share of flip exits fell to 25% from 48%, so the volume buyers who used to absorb assignments are a smaller part of the market. Recorded spreads compressed to a $19,575 median from $36,250, and only 7 assignments surfaced against 20 a year ago. The data suggests working the zips where flip share is rising — 44105 (120 buys, $45,000 median), 44108 ($50,000) and 44120 ($73,334) — and pricing contracts to the newer, smaller operators rather than to one repeat account.

Hold discipline is doing the work: 188 days and 74% over basis

Flippers
Median hold on flips sold this edition was 188 days versus 236 a year ago, with 24% under 90 days and 21% still over a year. Margin per deal ran $75,000 median, up from $62,000, on a resale median of $185,000 and $130/sqft against $140/sqft. Single-family exits cleared at a $68,822 median. Worth checking whether your last three projects beat the 188-day mark; the long-hold quintile is where the 9% of flippers who are net-negative lifetime tend to sit.

The low-basis east side is where the flip mix moved — expect company

Landlords
Slavic Village/Union-Miles went to 7.2% of flip exits from 4.7%, Shaker Square/Buckeye to 5.7% from 3.7%, Old Brooklyn to 4.5% from 2.3%. Those are the same $45,000–$101,000 median-buy zips landlords shop. Small multifamily rose to 16.7% of flips from 12.7%, so more two-to-fours are coming back to market finished and priced up. The alternative is unfinished multi at a $51/sqft basis versus $108/sqft single family, or the outer ring at Mentor $204/sqft and Willoughby $208/sqft.

Eleven recorded loans against 91 seller notes marks the open lane

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 11 (median $60,000) from 66 a year ago, while seller-carried notes rose to 91 (median $138,000) from 72. Sellers are filling the gap on terms lenders are not writing. With median flip hold at 188 days and the interquartile range 96 to 327 days, the data suggests demand for 9-to-12-month paper in the $60,000–$140,000 range on inner-city single family and two-to-fours, the collateral types where 58% of flip stock is pre-1950.

Retail and apartments are trading on price per foot, not per door

Commercial investors
Investors closed 81 retail/service parcels at a $324,000 median ($77/sqft), 40 apartment 5+ at $340,500 ($52/sqft), 32 industrial at $387,500 ($55/sqft) and 31 office at $460,000 ($96/sqft). Only 5 commercial flips sold, at a $90,000 median and $18/sqft, so there is little evidence of a repositioning bid at the small end. Mixed use printed the lowest basis at $32/sqft on 15 deals — worth checking against carry, since exits at that per-foot level are thin in the record.

Factoids

Numbers from this period a practitioner would repeat to a colleague. Each computed directly from recorded instruments.

30% of investor purchases — Out-of-state share of buyers, at a $160,100 median

holding steady · vs $140,000 median for local buyers
Out-of-area capital (43% counting the rest of Ohio) is paying above the local median, with California alone at 128 purchases, then Florida 76 and Michigan 52. Sellers with clean, rent-ready product have a bidder pool that is not local price memory.

91 seller-financed notes — New notes held, median $138,000

trending up · vs 72 a year ago, median $122,500
Seller paper is up 26% year over year while recorded private loans fell to 11 from 66. Where bank or hard-money terms do not clear, sellers are carrying the difference themselves.

$51/sqft on small multifamily — Median purchase price per square foot, multi-family

holding steady · vs $108/sqft for single family
Investors bought 277 multi-family parcels at a $103,000 median against 1,266 single-family at $135,050. The per-foot gap is where the basis advantage still lives for buy-and-hold.

1,468 units added — Units added by 767 local landlords since the last data drop

trending up · alongside 1,298 first-time local landlords appearing in the records
Accumulation is broad rather than concentrated, and 90 landlords also became note-holders this period. New entrants at this scale keep bid pressure on the $45,000–$75,000 east-side product.

7 wholesale assignments — Assignments surfacing on recorded deeds, median spread $19,575

trending down · vs 20 a year ago, median spread $36,250
Recorded spreads are roughly half what they were, and the count is a floor since most assignments never touch a deed. What surfaces suggests thinner middlemen economics on the low-basis inventory.

15 builder new-build sales — New construction closings at a $335,485 median

trending down · vs 71 a year ago, median $311,190
Volume is down roughly 79% year over year while the median ticket moved up. Builders remain active on lots — NVR at 42 parcels, mostly residential land in Medina — but delivered product is scarce.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
