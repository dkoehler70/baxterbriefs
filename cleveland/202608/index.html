August Edition — Pipeline Drain

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

240 vs 517
Flips sold this edition versus the same two months a year ago, a 54% decline

Flip exits fell to 240 from 517 a year ago — the flip pipeline is thinning out, not repricing

Recorded flip resales came in at 240 against 517 a year ago, and 406 in the prior period. Pricing did not break with volume: the median flip sale was $203,500 versus $191,000 a year ago, and the median margin on measured flips was $93,450 versus $91,500. The contraction is in participation and inventory — 190 distinct flippers sold this period versus 352 a year ago, and homes held by flippers stood at 5,214 in May versus 6,117 a year earlier. Recent months can still grow as late records arrive.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer flipper buyers on the list — requalify the book toward landlords

Wholesalers
Only 190 distinct flippers recorded a sale this period versus 352 a year ago, and surfaced assignment spreads fell to a $19,575 median from $36,250. The data suggests rebuilding the buyer list around landlords: 767 owners added 1,468 units since the last drop and 1,298 first-time local landlords appeared. Deep-basis zips like 44105 ($45,000 median buy) and 44108 ($50,000) are where that demand is concentrated; treat the 7 recorded assignments as a floor.

Price held at $203,500 — the constraint is turn time, not exit value

Flippers
Median flip sale rose to $203,500 from $191,000 and median margin to $93,450 from $91,500, but median hold stretched to 175 days from 168, with 17% of exits sitting over a year and only 23% turning inside 90 days. Worth underwriting to a nine-month hold rather than six. Buy-to-sale gaps are widest in 44120 ($67,500 median buy against a $154,000 median sale) and 44128 ($66,647 against $132,000).

The multi-family basis discount is the clearest edge this period

Landlords
Multi-family traded at $103,000 median and $51/sqft against $135,050 and $108/sqft for single family, and condos at $189,900 and $167/sqft. With flipper acquisitions down and investor purchase medians at $140,000 versus $152,000 a year ago, competition for tired small multi looks lighter. Cleveland's citywide $100,000 median and $70/sqft, and Euclid at $110,000 and $87/sqft, are where that basis is checkable against actual rent rolls.

Fill the gap seller paper is currently absorbing

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 8 from 56 a year ago at a $59,000 median, while seller-financed notes rose to 73 from 55 at $125,000. That spread suggests borrowers are being financed by sellers because outside terms are absent, not because deals stopped. With median flip holds at 175 days and 17% exceeding a year, term and extension structure matters more than rate; the active recorded lenders remain small, with the top name at 23 loans and a $95,000 median.

Apartment 5+ still clears near land pricing per foot

Multifamily investors
Forty Apartment 5+ purchases recorded at a $340,500 median and $52/sqft — essentially the same per-foot basis as unspecified multi-family at $51/sqft. Small multi also took a larger share of flip exits, 16.7% versus 14.3% a year ago, so renovated product is reaching the market. The data suggests comparing $52/sqft acquisitions against those flipped comps before paying up for turnkey; recorded counts still lag four to eight weeks.

Flip Pipeline — Buying, Selling and Inventory

Homes bought by known flippers and flips sold, per month, alongside the stock of houses sitting in flipper hands. A house enters inventory when a flipper buys it and leaves when the resale records — or when it passes 24 months unsold, at which point it stops being a flip in progress. That release is why inventory can fall in a month when buying outruns selling.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
