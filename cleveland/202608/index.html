August Edition — Exit Gap

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

288 sold vs 528 bought

Flippers kept buying and stopped selling: 288 recorded flip exits against 528 flipper purchases this edition

Flippers acquired 528 homes and recorded 288 exits this edition, a net build of 240 units against a net 191 a year ago, when 817 were bought and 626 sold. Exits are down 54% from a year ago and down 40% from the prior period, while the median flip sale held at $186,250 versus $180,000. Held inventory is absorbing the difference: median hold on completed flips was 192.5 days, a quarter of exits took over a year, and median margin percentage slipped to 82% from 87% even as median dollar margin rose to $82,950 from $77,000.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Flipper buy pace is holding; assignment paper is not

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds against 20 a year ago, and that count is a floor. Flippers still took down 528 homes this edition, so demand is there — the data suggests fitting the buy boxes that are actually clearing: 44105 at a $45,000 median buy, 44108 at $50,000, 44112 at $30,200. Spreads that did surface ran a $19,575 median, well below the $36,250 median a year ago, so worth pricing assignments against that band rather than last year's.

Model 6.3 months plus tail risk, and check the margin percentage, not the dollar

Flippers
Completed exits held a median 192.5 days, with the middle half at 3.5 to 11.8 months and 25% past a year. Median margin rose to $82,950 from $77,000 but margin percentage fell to 82% from 87%, so carrying cost is eating the gain. Single-family flip resales ran a $68,822 median at $49/sqft against a $108/sqft single-family purchase basis; worth re-underwriting exits in 44128, 44105 and 44109, where the active 3-to-8-flip cohort concentrates.

The unsold flip stack is a potential source of off-market inventory

Landlords
Flippers netted 240 more homes than they sold this edition versus 191 a year ago, and a quarter of exits are already past 12 months. Owners at that stage are candidates for a hold-to-hold trade. Multi-family purchases cleared at a $103,000 median and $51/sqft against $108/sqft for single family, so the per-foot basis argument still favors 2-4 unit product in Cleveland, where the citywide median buy was $100,000 at $70/sqft.

Recorded private loans are at 8; the rehab slot is being filled by national desks

Private lenders
Eight private loans were recorded this edition against 56 a year ago, at a $59,000 median. Meanwhile Kiavi funded 77 loans to 36 borrowers with 84% rehab-funded and 1.30 loan-to-price, and RFLF 4 funded 61 to 29 borrowers at 1.35. The data suggests the open lane is small-balance paper below the $123,000 median loan on 2-4 unit collateral, the product type behind four of the five largest recorded private loans this edition.

Apartment 5+ is priced near multi-family per-foot, not near single family

Multifamily investors
Forty Apartment 5+ purchases cleared at a $340,500 median and $52/sqft, essentially the same per-foot basis as the 277 multi-family purchases at $51/sqft, while single family ran $108/sqft. Flip exits on multi-family were 33 at a $50,000 median and $18/sqft, so distressed resale comps sit far below acquisition basis — worth verifying condition and unit count on any comp in that band before applying it.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
