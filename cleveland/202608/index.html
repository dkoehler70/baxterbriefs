August Edition — Cheaper Basis

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$140,000 vs $152,000
Median investor purchase price this edition against the same period a year ago, while the median flip resale rose to $186,250 from $180,000

Acquisition basis fell 8% while flip exits held: median investor buy $140,000 against a $186,250 flip resale median

The buy side repriced and the sell side did not. Median investor purchase price came in at $140,000 against $152,000 a year ago, while the median flip resale was $186,250 versus $180,000. Median gross margin per completed flip widened to $82,950 from $77,000, and median hold time shortened to 192.5 days from 202.5. Margins are gross — rehab and carry are not public record — and the percentage margin narrowed to 82% from 87%, so the dollar gain comes from cheaper entries rather than stronger exits.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Aged holdings, not fresh listings, look like the live seller list

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds at a $19,575 median spread, against 20 at $36,250 a year ago, so buyer competition on marketed paper is thin. With 137 held flips aging out in the latest month and operator count down to 224 from 429, the data suggests working the holders of 2025-vintage inventory rather than the retail funnel. Cheapest recorded basis sits in 44105 ($45,000 median buy) and 44108 ($50,000).

Underwrite the entry, not the exit, at a $140,000 market basis

Flippers
Median gross margin of $82,950 (vs $77,000 a year ago) came from a $140,000 buy median, not from resale strength — resale per foot was $133/sqft versus $134/sqft a year ago and sale-to-assessed slipped to 1.44 from 1.49. Worth checking 44120, where a $67,500 median buy sat against a $122,500 median sale, and budgeting to the 192.5-day median hold. Margins here are gross; rehab and carry are not in the record.

The per-foot gap still favours small multi

Landlords
Investors recorded 277 multi-family purchases at a $103,000 median and $51/sqft, against $108/sqft on 1,266 single-family buys. 767 local landlords added units since the last drop (+1,468 units) and 1,298 first-time local landlords appeared. If you are buying for yield rather than resale, the data suggests screening 44105, 44128 and 44108, where median buys ran $45,000–$67,000 this edition.

Small-balance rehab paper is the unfilled slot

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 8 at a $59,000 median, from 56 at $100,000 a year ago, while trailing-12-month financed share held at 55.9% of observed purchases and 715 deals carried rehab funding. The largest institutional book, Kiavi, wrote 77 loans at a $150,500 median with 84% rehab share and a 1.30 loan-to-price. The gap the data shows is under $100,000 on 2-4 units, where recorded private loans this edition clustered at $93,000 and below.

Apartment 5+ trades near small-multi per-foot pricing

Multifamily investors
40 Apartment 5+ purchases recorded at a $340,500 median and $52/sqft, essentially level with the $51/sqft on 277 multi-family purchases — scale is not carrying a per-foot premium in the recorded set. Small multi also made 17.7% of flip exits, up from 14.9% a year ago, so expect renovated 2-4 unit product competing with your acquisitions. Recorded counts lag 4-8 weeks and the newest month can still grow.

Roughly half as many operators cleared a resale, yet the top ten still took only 18.2% of flip revenue versus 19.9% a year ago. Exits are thinner across the board rather than concentrating in a few shops.

trending up · vs 2.7% a year ago
The zip recorded 75 investor purchases at a $67,500 median buy against a $122,500 median sale. It is the largest single share gain among zips this edition; 44137 (Maple Heights) went the other way, from 2.9% to none.

$51/sqft — Median price per square foot on 2-4 unit purchases (277 deals, $103,000 median)

holding steady · vs $108/sqft on single family this edition
Small multi keeps trading at roughly half the single-family basis per foot, with condos at $167/sqft. For landlords the cheapest recorded square footage is still in 2-4 units and Apartment 5+ at $52/sqft.

31% out of state — Share of investor purchases by out-of-state buyers, median $152,500

holding steady · vs 56% local at a $132,000 median
Out-of-area capital took 44% of investor purchases in total and pays a higher median than local buyers. Local operators are still winning the cheaper end of the recorded deal flow.

8 private loans — Recorded private loans, median $59,000

trending down · vs 56 at a $100,000 median a year ago
Recorded private debt has nearly vanished while seller-financed notes rose to 73 from 55 a year ago at a $125,000 median. Sellers, not private lenders, are carrying more of the paper that appears in the records.

9,133 held flips — Estimated flipper-held inventory in the latest month

trending down · vs 9,948 a year earlier
The pipeline is draining, but 137 held properties aged out in the latest month, the highest monthly figure in the series since 2024. That is the pool most likely to come back to market at a discount.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
