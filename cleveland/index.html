August Edition — Basis Slippage

Analyzing transactions recorded April 1 – May 31, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$145,000 vs $155,000
Median investor purchase price this edition against the same period a year ago; flip exit median was $71,100 vs $71,250

Investor acquisition basis has slipped to $145,000 while flip exits sit flat at $71,100

The buy side got cheaper and the sell side did not move. Investors paid a median $145,000 this edition against $155,000 a year ago, on 2,146 purchases versus 2,212 — volume essentially held, pricing did not. Resale flips closed at a median $71,100, statistically unchanged from $71,250 a year ago, so the margin improvement is coming entirely from acquisition, not exit. Within Cleveland proper the median purchase was $106,000 at $70 per square foot. Recorded counts lag four to eight weeks and the most recent month can still grow by roughly 15%.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price contracts to a $18,000 spread, not last year's $37,500

Wholesalers
The median surfaced spread halved to $18,000 while assignment count held at 15. Buyer depth is clearest in the low-basis east side zips — 44105 at a $54,000 median buy on 135 purchases, 44108 at $71,000 on 59, 44128 at $72,000 on 78. The data suggests writing offers that assume a five-figure spread and a landlord end-buyer rather than a flipper, since flip resale medians have not risen to fund a fatter assignment.

Margin has to come from the buy at $145,000, because exits are flat

Flippers
Resale flips cleared a median $71,100 against $71,250 a year ago while the market's acquisition median fell to $145,000 from $155,000. Single-family exits at $78,450 and $56/sqft remain the workable product; 2–4 unit exits at $39,450 ($18/sqft) and condo exits at $32,000 do not support rehab budgets. The hold-time baseline is a median 5.2 months with the middle half at 3.1–8.3 months, so it is worth underwriting carry to roughly two quarters rather than 90 days.

The cheapest basis per foot is still 2–4 unit at $58/sqft

Landlords
Small multifamily traded at a $115,000 median and $58/sqft across 284 purchases, versus $103/sqft for single family. Outer-ring inventory offers a middle path: Euclid at $115,000 and $81/sqft on 69 deals, Maple Heights at $99,000 and $95/sqft on 44, Lorain at $110,000 on 61. With 767 landlords adding 1,468 units this period, competition for the sub-$120,000 band is real — worth checking whether the per-foot discount survives your rehab scope.

Twelve recorded loans leaves the rehab band open

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 12 from 71 a year ago, and the ones that printed skewed large at a $179,500 median. The gap is in the $50,000–$120,000 rehab band in 44105, 44108 and 44128, where flip exits cluster near $71,100 and bank product is thin — banks and REO accounted for only 31 purchases. Seller notes at 80, up from 72, show sellers already filling part of that void; pricing against carried paper is the relevant comparison now.

Retail and industrial per-foot basis stayed the lowest in the market

Commercial investors
Retail and service traded at a $264,684 median and $57/sqft on 70 purchases, office at $400,000 and $73/sqft on 34, industrial at $395,000 and $37/sqft on 34, and Apartment 5+ at $252,500 and $54/sqft on 30. Those per-foot levels sit below single-family residential at $103/sqft. Only four retail flips resold, at a $264,369 median, so the data suggests hold-oriented underwriting rather than a resale exit in this class.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated April 1 – May 31, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
