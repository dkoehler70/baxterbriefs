August Edition — Basis Gap

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$52 vs $108 per sqft
Median price per square foot on 2–4 unit investor purchases this edition, against single-family purchases in the same period

Rental stock is trading at half the single-family basis: 2–4 unit purchases cleared $52 per square foot against $108 for single family

Investor buying held its level at 2,186 purchases, but the per-foot basis split sharply by product. The 286 two-to-four unit deals cleared at $52 per square foot on a $103,000 median, against $108 per square foot and $135,858 for the 1,256 single-family purchases. Apartment 5+ traded at the same $52 per foot on a $340,500 median, so the discount runs across rental stock rather than small multi alone. Flipped 2–4 units resold at a $50,000 median versus $68,822 for single family, which suggests the cheaper foot is being bought for cash flow, not resale.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Source 2–4 unit stock on the east side, where the per-foot discount is deepest

Wholesalers
The 286 two-to-four unit trades cleared at a $103,000 median and $52 per square foot, and the highest-volume zips remain 44105 (119 buys, $45,000 median), 44108 (62 buys, $50,000) and 44128 (68 buys, $67,546). With recorded assignment spreads down to a $19,575 median from $36,250 a year ago, it is worth checking whether your fee assumptions still hold in that band, and building the buyer list toward out-of-state accounts that took 31% of purchases at a $160,000 median.

Exit prices are running below acquisition basis per foot — underwrite the resale, not the purchase

Flippers
Flip exits cleared a $62,500 median, with single-family flips at $68,822 and $50 per square foot against a $108 per square foot single-family acquisition basis. Condo exits at $52,900 and 2–4 unit exits at $50,000 are thinner still. The latest measurable hold period ran a median 5.2 months, with 12% over a year, so the data suggests pricing a carry allowance of at least two quarters into any deal underwritten at this edition's exit medians.

The cheap foot is in Euclid, Lorain and Maple Heights, not in Cleveland's inner ring alone

Landlords
Cleveland purchases ran 1,114 at a $105,000 median and $68 per square foot; Euclid at $86, Maple Heights at $92, Lorain at $77 and Elyria at $78 per square foot sit close behind on smaller volume. With 2–4 unit product at $52 per square foot against $108 for single family, it is worth checking whether the operating cost differential on small multi is genuinely worth the roughly half-price basis in your submarket.

Seller paper is filling the gap you are no longer recording

Private lenders
Only 11 private loans recorded at a $60,000 median this edition, against 64 at $130,418 a year ago, while seller-financed notes rose to 91 at a $138,000 median. The lending that is visible skews small and commercial — the largest recorded loans were $327,617 on retail in Brunswick and $159,000 on office in Fairport Harbor. The data suggests unmet demand in the $50,000–$130,000 bridge band against 2–4 unit collateral bought near a $103,000 median.

Apartment 5+ prices at the same $52 per foot as small multi, while builder supply has stalled

Commercial investors
Forty apartment 5+ trades cleared at a $340,500 median and $52 per square foot, level with 2–4 unit product; retail/service ran 78 at $314,750 ($78/sqft), office 32 at $538,750 ($96/sqft) and industrial 31 at $375,000 ($50/sqft). Builder new-build sales fell to 12 from 69 a year ago at a $324,700 median, so competing new rental supply is thin. Worth checking whether existing 5+ product at $52 per foot underwrites better than lot-and-build in the outer counties.

Factoids

Numbers from this period a practitioner would repeat to a colleague. Each computed directly from recorded instruments.

31% out of state — Out-of-state share of investor purchases, at a $160,000 median

trending up · vs 58% local buying at a $136,750 median
Out-of-area capital took 672 of 2,186 purchases and paid roughly $23,000 more at the median than local buyers. California led feeder states with 128 purchases, ahead of Florida at 76 and Michigan at 52.

2,186 purchases at $150,000 — Investor purchases and median price this edition

holding steady · vs 2,255 at $155,500 a year ago
The acquisition side is essentially unchanged in count and about 4% softer at the median. Deal flow, not buyer appetite, is the constraint on the buy side right now.

91 seller-financed notes — New notes held, median $138,000

trending up · vs 72 at $122,500 a year ago
Seller paper is up 26% year over year and up 14% from the prior period, with note size rising as well. Sellers of rental stock are increasingly carrying the paper themselves.

11 recorded private loans — Private loans recorded, median $60,000

trending down · vs 64 at a $130,418 median a year ago
Recorded private lending has all but vanished at the small end, and the loans that do record are less than half their prior size. Borrowers appear to be sourcing outside the recorder or taking seller paper instead.

1,468 units added by 767 landlords — Net units added by local landlords since the last data drop

trending up · alongside 1,298 first-time local landlords appearing in the records
Portfolio growth is broad rather than concentrated, averaging under two units per growing owner. New entrants continue to arrive at the bottom of the price band.

7 wholesale assignments — Assignments surfacing on recorded deeds, median spread $19,575

trending down · vs 20 at a $36,250 median spread a year ago
Recorded assignment volume is a floor, since most assignments never touch a deed, but both the count and the visible spread have compressed. Where paper does record, the margin is roughly half what it was.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
