August Edition — Exit Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

225 vs 388
Flips resold this edition versus the same two months a year ago, with the median sale at $62,500 versus $74,650

Flip exits thinned and repriced while acquisitions held: 225 resales at $62,500 against 388 at $74,650 a year ago

Flip exits thinned across the region: 225 recorded resales against 388 a year ago and 296 in the prior period. Price moved with volume — the median flip sale was $62,500 versus $74,650 a year ago, and per-foot proceeds fell to $45.92 from $55.07. The acquisition side did not follow: 2,186 investor purchases at a $150,000 median, within 3% of last year's 2,255. Exits, not entries, are where this market tightened. Single-family flips, 170 of the 225, cleared a $68,822 median at $50 per square foot.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price contracts to a $62,500 exit, not last year's $74,650

Wholesalers
With the median flip resale at $62,500 and flip $/sqft at $45.92, buy-box math that assumed a year-ago exit no longer clears. The data suggests keeping assignments in the high-volume, low-basis zips — 44105 at a $45,000 median buy, 44108 at $50,000, 44120 at $73,334 — where 12, 11 and 8 flips respectively still found exits. Only 7 assignments surfaced on deeds at a $19,575 median spread, so expect fewer competing contracts but also fewer end buyers; verifying the buyer is one of the 202 active flippers is worth the call.

Underwrite carry to a 124-day median, not a 90-day plan

Flippers
A quarter of measured exits this period took more than a year against an 11.8% baseline share, and the middle half ran 2.9 to 12.0 months. That argues for budgeting carry well past 90 days on anything east of downtown. Two- to four-unit flips are the weakest corner — 33 sold at a $50,000 median and $18/sqft — while single family held $68,822 and $50/sqft on 170 sales. Worth checking 44128, 44109, 44110 and 44108, each showing 11-12 flips this period, before adding inventory elsewhere.

The buy side stayed open while flippers stepped back

Landlords
Investor purchases held at 2,186 with a $150,000 median, so acquisition competition has not eased in dollar terms, but it has thinned among flip buyers. Two- to four-unit stock traded at a $103,000 median and $52/sqft, roughly half the $107.69/sqft single-family basis. For scale at low entry, the data points to Cleveland at $105,000 and $68/sqft, Lorain at $100,000 and $77/sqft, and Maple Heights at $92,000. With 767 owners adding 1,468 units since the prior drop, expect steady bidding on turnkey rentals from out-of-state buyers paying a $160,000 median.

Fill the gap seller carry is currently taking

Private lenders
Only 11 private loans were recorded at a $60,000 median, against 64 a year ago, while 91 new seller-financed notes appeared at a $138,000 median. Sellers are absorbing paper that used to be lender business, generally on higher-priced product. Given that 25% of flips now take over a year to exit, terms and extension pricing matter more than headline rate; note that flip margins in the measured sample ran a $15,250 negative median, so collateral value, not sponsor projections, is the safer anchor. Most active recorded lender remains Collins Larry at 23 loans.

Small commercial basis sits near multifamily levels per foot

Commercial investors
Retail and service traded at 78 deals, a $314,750 median and $77.61/sqft; office at 32 deals and $538,750 ($96.48/sqft); industrial at 31 deals and $375,000 ($50.07/sqft); Apartment 5+ at 40 deals, $340,500 and $51.96/sqft. Commercial flip exits are essentially untested — five retail resales at a $90,000 median and $18.08/sqft. The data suggests hold-oriented underwriting rather than resale in that lane. Builder new-build sales fell to 12 from 69 a year ago, which thins near-term competition for residential land as well.

202 flippers — Distinct sellers behind this edition's 225 flips

trending down · vs 304 behind 388 flips a year ago
The pool of active flippers shrank faster than deal count, and the top ten took 22.9% of flips versus 19.7% a year ago. Fewer, larger operators are absorbing what exit volume remains.

25% held over a year — Share of measured flips with a hold longer than 365 days

trending up · vs 11.8% in the trailing-year baseline
Hold times have gone barbell: 27% turned in under 90 days against a 21.5% baseline, while a quarter sat more than twelve months. Median measured hold was 124 days on 88 of 225 flips.

31% out of state — Out-of-state share of investor purchases, at a $160,000 median

holding steady · vs $136,750 median for the 58% local share
Out-of-area capital keeps buying a higher-priced product than local buyers do, led by California at 128 purchases and Florida at 76. Local operators are still winning the cheaper end of the stock.

11 private loans vs 91 seller notes — Recorded private loans against new seller-financed notes

trending down · private loans were 64 a year ago; notes were 72
Recorded hard-money volume has all but vanished while seller carry rose 26% year over year to a $138,000 median. Sellers, not lenders, are financing a growing share of recorded transfers.

$19,575 median spread — Median assignment spread on the 7 wholesale deals that surfaced on a deed

trending down · vs $36,250 on 20 assignments a year ago
Both count and spread compressed, consistent with thinner flipper demand at the buy end. Treat the count as a floor — most assignments never touch a recorded deed.

119 buys at $45,000 — Investor purchases and median price in 44105 (Slavic Village / Union-Miles)

trending up · vs 80 buys at a $222,500 median in Mentor 44060
The busiest zip in the region is also its cheapest large one, and it produced 12 flips this period. Volume is concentrating where basis is lowest even as exit pricing softens.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
