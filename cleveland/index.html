August Edition — Multifamily Basis

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$51/sqft vs $108/sqft
Median price per square foot on small multifamily purchases this edition, against single-family purchases in the same period

Investors are buying small multifamily square footage at half the single-family basis: $51/sqft on 277 purchases vs $108/sqft on 1,266 houses

Investors paid a median $103,000 for 277 small multifamily buildings this edition, $51 per square foot against $108 per square foot for the 1,266 single-family purchases. The discount is not a 2–4-unit quirk: Apartment 5+ cleared 40 deals at $52 per square foot, while condos ran $167 per square foot on 264 purchases. Flip resales tracked the same shift, with small multifamily at 17.6% of exits versus 14.5% a year ago. The cheap square footage sits mostly inside Cleveland proper, where the citywide median buy was $100,000 at $70 per square foot.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Contract the cheap square footage, not the cheap houses

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds at a $19,575 median spread, down from 20 at $36,250 a year ago — treat that as a floor, but the thinning is real. The data suggests the deepest buyer bench sits where basis is lowest: 44105 (111 buys, $45,000 median), 44108 (59 buys, $50,000) and 44128 (62 buys, $66,646). With 277 small multifamily purchases clearing at $51/sqft, 2–4 unit product is worth working before another vacant single-family in the same block.

Price the multifamily discount as work, not free equity

Flippers
Single family made up 170 of 290 flip resales this edition; small multifamily was 33 at $18/sqft in the resale mix, the lowest basis and, in practice, the heaviest scope. Median hold ran 190 days with 24% of exits over a year, so worth underwriting carry at four to six months minimum. Where buy-to-sale spread has been widest, 44120 (18 flips, $67,500 buy vs $122,500 sale) and 44128 ($66,646 vs $132,000) are the sheets to check first.

The 2–4 unit entry is half the per-foot cost of a condo

Landlords
Small multifamily traded at a median $103,000 and $51/sqft this edition, against $135,050 and $108/sqft for single family and $189,900 and $167/sqft for condos. With 767 local owners adding 1,468 units since the last drop, competition for that product is rising, so the data suggests moving on inspections quickly. Cleveland proper, at a $100,000 median and $70/sqft citywide, is where the per-foot gap is widest.

Small-balance multifamily rehab paper is under-served

Private lenders
Just 8 private loans were recorded this period at a $59,000 median, against 56 a year ago, while seller notes rose to 73. Three of the five largest recorded loans this edition were on 2–4 unit buildings at $93,000 to $100,000 — the size band the national shops largely skip, given Kiavi's $158,200 median at a 1.29 loan-to-price. Worth checking whether $50,000–$100,000 rehab notes on 44105, 44108 and 44120 multifamily fit your risk box; recorded counts lag 4–8 weeks, so current demand may be understated.

Apartment 5+ prices at the same per-foot level as 2–4 units

Multifamily investors
Forty Apartment 5+ purchases cleared at a $340,500 median and $52/sqft, essentially the same per-foot basis as the 277 small multifamily buys at $51/sqft — meaning size is not currently commanding a premium or a discount per foot. Larger recorded deals clustered in Cleveland, including a $2.6M 11,152 sqft building on W 20th and a $1.7M four-parcel package on Ridge Rd. Debt for this product is coming from a short list of local names; Fairmount Farms II wrote 3 loans totaling $4.8M against Cleveland apartments over the trailing year.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
