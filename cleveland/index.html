August Edition — Exit Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

225 vs 388
Flips sold this edition against 388 in the same two months a year ago, with the median flip price at $62,500 vs $74,650

Flip exits have thinned to 225 from 388 a year ago and are clearing 16% cheaper, while the acquisition side holds its bid at a $150,000 median

Recorded flip resales came in at 225 against 388 a year ago, and the median flip cleared $62,500 versus $74,650 — fewer exits at lower prices. The buy side did not follow: 2,186 investor purchases at a $150,000 median, up from 2,113 at $142,000 in the prior period. Single-family flips resold at a $50/sqft median while investor single-family acquisitions ran $108/sqft, so the spread is being paid on the way in, not out. Monthly flips fell from 127 in May to 98 in June, though the most recent month can still grow up to 15% on backfill.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Re-aim the buyer list from flippers to landlords

Wholesalers
With flip exits at 225 vs 388 a year ago and recorded assignment spreads at a $19,575 median vs $36,250 a year ago, the flipper end of the list is paying less. The data suggests the deepest live demand sits in 44105 (119 buys, $45,000 median), 44128 (68 buys, $67,547) and 44108 (62 buys, $50,000) — sub-$70,000 stock that buy-and-hold accounts absorb. Worth checking whether your contracts price to a rental yield buyer rather than an ARV buyer.

Underwrite a longer hold and a lower exit

Flippers
The median flip cleared $62,500 this edition against $74,650 a year ago, and single-family exits ran $50/sqft. Hold data from the latest measurable period shows a 5.2-month median, with the middle half at 3.1–8.3 months and 12% over a year. Worth stress-testing deals at an eight-month hold and last-period pricing rather than a year-ago comp, particularly on 2–4 unit rehabs, where flip resales showed a $50,000 median at $18/sqft.

The buy window is where flippers are stepping back

Landlords
767 local landlords added 1,468 units since the prior drop, and 2–4 unit product traded at a $103,000 median ($52/sqft) against $135,858 ($108/sqft) for single family. With flip exits thinning, the data suggests more motivated sellers of half-finished rehab stock in 44120 ($73,334 median buy), 44128 ($67,547) and 44110 ($87,500). Verify condition and scope carefully — the discount on that footage reflects unfinished work.

Compete against seller paper, not against banks

Private lenders
Only 11 private loans recorded this period at a $60,000 median, while 91 seller-financed notes were created at a $138,000 median. That gap is the opportunity: sellers are carrying paper because bridge capital is not showing up at closing. The most active recorded lender, Collins Larry, did 23 loans at a $95,000 median. A short-duration product sized near the $60,000–$100,000 band, underwritten to a $62,500 flip exit rather than a year-ago comp, is what the record shows is missing.

Footage is cheapest in apartments and industrial

Commercial investors
Apartment 5+ traded at 40 deals, a $340,500 median and $52/sqft; industrial at 31 deals, $375,000 and $50/sqft; retail/service at 78 deals, $314,750 and $78/sqft; office at 32 deals, $538,750 and $96/sqft. The data suggests the per-foot basis argument favors apartments and industrial over office at current recorded pricing. Small mixed-use was thin at 15 deals ($155,000 median, $32/sqft), so comps there are weak.

Factoids

Numbers from this period a practitioner would repeat to a colleague. Each computed directly from recorded instruments.

31% out-of-state — Share of investor purchases by out-of-state buyers, 672 deals at a $160,000 median

holding steady · vs 58% local at a $136,750 median
Out-of-area capital is buying up-market relative to local buyers, with CA (128), FL (76) and MI (52) the top feeders. Local operators bidding under $140,000 are largely competing with each other, not with the coastal money.

91 seller-financed notes — New seller-held notes recorded, median $138,000

trending up · vs 72 a year ago, median $122,500
Seller paper is up 26% year over year and the typical note is larger. Sellers who cannot get a clean cash exit are carrying the paper themselves, which shows up as a slower, financed transfer of inventory rather than a stalled one.

11 private loans — Recorded private mortgage originations, median $60,000

trending down · vs 64 a year ago, median $130,418
Recorded hard-money volume has all but disappeared and the loans that do record are half the prior size. Some financing has moved off the deed record, but the visible bridge market for rehabs is thin.

7 assignments — Wholesale assignments surfacing on recorded deeds, median spread $19,575

trending down · vs 20 a year ago, median spread $36,250
Counts are a floor since most assignments never touch a deed, but both the count and the spread have compressed. The buyer pool that pays a five-figure assignment fee is smaller than it was.

119 buys in 44105 — Investor purchases in Slavic Village / Union-Miles, median $45,000, 12 flips

trending up · vs 93 buys in 44102 at a $115,000 median
The single most active zip in the region is also the cheapest of the top tier. Volume is concentrating where the basis is under $50,000, which is a landlord and note-buyer profile more than a retail-flip profile.

12 new-build sales — Builder new-construction closings, median $324,700

trending down · vs 69 a year ago, median $322,900
New-build delivery has effectively stopped as a source of competing inventory at the $300,000-plus level, while price per unit held flat. Builders are still buying lots — residential lots ran 114 purchases at a $164,500 median.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
