August Edition — Thinning Exits

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

287 vs 619
Flips sold this edition against the same period a year ago, with investor purchases nearly unchanged at 2,022 vs 2,033

Flip resales ran at half last year's pace — 287 vs 619 — while investor buying held flat and flip pricing did not break

Flippers recorded 287 resales this edition against 619 a year ago, a 54% drop, while investor purchases were essentially flat at 2,022 vs 2,033. The squeeze is on the exit, not the entry: the flipper cohort bought 488 properties against the 287 it resold, and tracked flip inventory sits at 8,196 vs 8,960 a year ago. Pricing has not broken — median flip resale $185,000 vs $179,000, median margin $82,900 vs $77,000. Fewer operators are clearing exits; those who clear are not discounting. Recorded counts lag 4-8 weeks and the most recent month can grow up to about 15%.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Buy-side demand is still there; assignment paper is not showing up

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds, median spread $19,575, against 20 a year ago at $36,250 — and that count is a floor. Yet flippers bought 488 against 287 resales, so acquisition appetite persists. The data suggests working the cheap-basis zips where flipper buying is thickest: 44105 (111 buys, $45,000 median) and 44108 (59 buys, $50,000 median), and pricing spreads closer to this period's $19,575 than to last year's.

Underwrite the exit queue, not the exit price

Flippers
Median resale held at $185,000 vs $179,000 a year ago and median margin rose to $82,900 from $77,000, so the price side is intact; the problem is that only 287 exits cleared against 619. With a 191-day median hold and 24% of exits past a year, it is worth carrying 352 days of cost — this period's 75th percentile — in the model, and checking how many comparable resales a target zip actually produced before committing.

Aging flip stock and a lower purchase median favor patient buyers

Landlords
Investor purchase median fell to $140,000 from $152,000 a year ago, and Cleveland's 1,040 purchases came in at a $100,000 median, roughly $70/sqft, against $205/sqft in Mentor and $217/sqft in Willoughby. With 767 local owners adding 1,468 units since the last drop, competition is real but priced. Flippers holding past 365 days — a quarter of this period's exits — are the sellers worth approaching directly.

Fill the gap the deed record shows: short bridge on 6-12 month holds

Private lenders
Eight recorded private loans this period, median $59,000, against 56 a year ago, while seller notes rose to 73. Over the trailing year, private loans (972) still outnumbered bank loans (705) at a 1.06 median loan-to-price. The data suggests demand sits in the sub-$100,000 Cleveland basis range and in terms sized to the 191-day median hold, with the 352-day tail priced as extension risk rather than ignored.

Small multi still trades far below house $/sqft, and flips out of it are thin

Multifamily investors
Investors bought 277 multi-family parcels at a $103,000 median ($51/sqft) and 40 Apartment 5+ at $340,500 ($52/sqft), against $135,050 and $108/sqft on 1,266 single-family. Only 33 multi-family flips cleared this period at a $50,000 median, so the resale channel out of this product is narrow. Worth checking whether hold-and-lease math, not resale, carries the deal before bidding at the $51/sqft level.

Flip Pipeline — Buying, Selling and Inventory

A house enters inventory when a flipper buys it and leaves on the next recorded sale — at any price, on any timeline, which is a wider net than the flip count elsewhere in this brief. A purchase stops counting after five years unsold, on the view that by then it is a hold rather than a flip in progress.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
