August Edition — Outside Money

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$160,100 vs $140,000
Median investor purchase price, out-of-state buyers (676 deals) versus local buyers (1,279 deals), this edition

Out-of-state buyers took 30% of investor purchases and paid $160,100 against locals' $140,000 — and almost none of it is institutional

Out-of-state buyers recorded 676 investor purchases this edition, 30% of the total, at a $160,100 median against $140,000 for the 1,279 local buys. California alone sent 128 buyers, ahead of Florida's 76 and Michigan's 52. Almost none of this is institutional: SFR funds recorded 12 purchases and iBuyers 23, while investor-tagged buyers took 92% of all deals. Total investor buying was essentially flat at 2,250 against 2,265 a year ago, but the median fell to $148,450 from $155,500. The mix of who is buying, not the volume, is what moved.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Seven recorded assignments; the buyer list matters more than the pipeline

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds (median spread $19,575) against 20 a year ago, and that count is a floor. With out-of-state buyers at 30% of purchases and a $160,100 median, it is worth testing whether your list is weighted toward the CA (128 buys), FL (76) and MI (52) feeders rather than local rehabbers. Sourcing at the 44105 ($45,000 median) and 44108 ($50,000) price points still produces the most deed volume.

Underwrite the six-month hold, not the ninety-day one

Flippers
Median hold on this period's exits was 172.5 days with the upper quartile at 284, and single family exits carried a $68,822 median. The data suggests budgeting carry through two quarters and checking whether your target zip actually clears: 44128 (11 flips on 68 buys), 44108 (11 on 61) and 44110 (11 on 46) had the highest flip-to-buy ratios this edition.

The small multifamily basis gap is the widest line in the table

Landlords
Small multifamily bought at a $103,000 median and $51/sqft against $135,050 and $108/sqft on single family. Cleveland proper ran $104,000 at $68/sqft versus Euclid at $115,000 and $86/sqft, so the per-foot spread is worth pricing against your own rent assumptions. 767 landlords added 1,468 units since the last drop, so competition for the same stock is visible in the record.

Sellers are writing the paper you are not

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 11 (median $60,000) from 66 a year ago while seller-carried notes rose to 91 at a $138,000 median. That gap points at small-balance rehab debt on $45,000–$115,000 basis product in 44105, 44108 and 44120 as the space currently filled by sellers. Sizing to the observed 172.5-day median hold, rather than a 90-day term, matches how these deals actually resolve.

Per-foot pricing splits sharply by asset class

Commercial investors
Retail/service printed 81 deals at a $324,000 median and $77/sqft, apartment 5+ 40 deals at $340,500 and $52/sqft, industrial 32 at $387,500 and $55/sqft, and office 31 at $460,000 and $96/sqft. The apartment and industrial per-foot marks sit near the small multifamily basis, which is worth checking before assuming a class premium. One $6.8M office record was excluded as implausible, so treat single large office comps carefully.

Exit volume is less than half of last year's while the median flip sale rose to $209,450 from $191,500. Recent months can still grow up to about 15% as late records arrive, so read the level, not the last data point.

holding steady · middle half 94 to 284 days
About 24% of flips turned in under 90 days and 17% took more than a year. Underwriting a two-quarter carry rather than a 90-day exit matches what the recorded pairs actually show.

trending down · vs $108/sqft on single family (1,266 deals)
Multi-family traded at a $103,000 median against $135,050 for single family, and at roughly half the price per foot. The gap is the cheapest square-foot basis in the residential set outside land.

trending up · vs 72 a year ago
Median note size rose to $138,000 from $122,500 while recorded private loans fell to 11 from 66. Sellers are carrying paper in a period when third-party private debt barely shows up in the record.

The count of distinct flippers selling fell to 193 from 355, but concentration fell much faster (HHI 98 vs 3,037). Fewer operators, but no one shop is setting comps in a given submarket.

120 buys in 44105 at $45,000 — Slavic Village / Union-Miles, the top investor purchase zip

The two busiest zips sit at opposite ends of basis, and 44105 produced 12 flips against 44102's five. Low-basis east side volume is still where the deal count is.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
