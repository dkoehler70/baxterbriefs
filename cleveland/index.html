August Edition — Flip Exits

This Issue August 2026 edition

214 vs 373
Flips sold this edition vs. the same period last year, while investor purchases held at 2,153 vs 2,220

Flip exits have thinned by 43% while investor buying holds near last year's pace

Recorded flip exits fell to 214 from 373 a year ago and from 288 in the prior period, while investor purchases held at 2,153 against 2,220 a year ago. The exits that did close were cheaper: median $62,100 versus $74,700 a year ago, with single-family flips clearing $49/sqft against a $107/sqft single-family purchase basis. The constraint is showing up on the resale side, not the acquisition side. Counts lag 4–8 weeks and June's 95 flips can still grow up to about 15% as late records arrive.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price to landlord math, not flip math

Wholesalers
With 6 assignments surfacing at a $19,575 median spread against 24 at $37,500 a year ago, and flip exits down to 214 from 373, the flipper bid is thinner. The data suggests routing low-basis east side product — 44105 at a $45,000 median buy, 44108 at $50,000, 44120 at $73,334 — toward the 767 landlords who added 1,468 units this period, and underwriting spreads closer to the current $19,575 median.

Check the gap between a $107/sqft basis and a $49/sqft exit

Flippers
Median flip exit fell to $62,100 from $74,700 a year ago, and single-family flips cleared $49/sqft while single-family purchases ran $107/sqft. Worth re-testing acquisition offers against that exit, particularly in the zips carrying the most flip volume — 44128 (11 flips on 68 buys), 44108 (11 on 62), 44109 (11 on 60), 44110 (11 on 46). Hold time on the last measurable cohort ran a median 5.3 months, with 13% over a year.

2–4 unit basis is the standout at $51/sqft

Landlords
Small multifamily traded 286 times at a $103,000 median and $51/sqft, against $107/sqft for the 1,246 single-family purchases. With flip exits down 43% year over year, some of that product is likely to reach the market unfinished; the data suggests testing bids on stalled rehabs in 44105, 44120 and 44128, where median buys sit between $45,000 and $73,334.

Fill the small 2–4 unit ticket the record shows missing

Private lenders
Only 11 private loans recorded at a $60,000 median, against 62 at $128,000 a year ago, while 90 seller-financed notes recorded at a $134,000 median. The loans that did record cluster small and on 2–4 unit Cleveland collateral — $100,000 on Stoughton Ave, $93,000 on E 132nd St, $93,000 on W 81st St. Worth checking whether borrowers in that band are taking seller paper because bridge capital is not being offered.

Per-foot basis remains lowest in industrial and 5+ apartment

Commercial investors
Retail/service traded 74 times at a $314,750 median ($77/sqft), Apartment 5+ 40 times at $340,500 ($52/sqft), and industrial 30 times at $373,500 ($45/sqft). The largest recorded purchases this period were commercial — $8.0M retail on Som Center Rd and $7.5M industrial on E 49th St. With builder new-build closings at 9 versus 50 a year ago, residential-lot competition from builders is materially lighter than last year.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
