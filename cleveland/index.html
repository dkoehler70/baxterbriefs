August Edition — Basis Gap

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$52 vs $108/sqft
Median price per square foot investors paid for 2–4 unit buildings versus single family purchases this edition

Investors are paying $52/sqft for 2–4 unit buildings against $108/sqft for single family — and the resale market is not paying that footage back

Investors closed 1,256 single-family purchases at a $135,858 median, or $108/sqft, and 286 two-to-four-unit purchases at $103,000, or $52/sqft. The discount does not carry to the exit: the 33 multifamily flips sold this edition cleared a $50,000 median at $18/sqft, against $68,822 and $50/sqft on the 170 single-family flips. The same split shows geographically — Cleveland proper absorbed 1,114 purchases at $105,000 and $68/sqft, while Mentor cleared $204/sqft and Willoughby $217/sqft. Cheap square footage is being bought for hold-side yield, not for resale value.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Build the list around $52/sqft product, not around square footage

Wholesalers
Only 7 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds this edition at a $19,575 median spread, versus 20 a year ago — the recorded channel is a floor, but the buy-side demand is legible. The data suggests the deepest buyer pool sits in 44105 (119 buys, $45,000 median), 44120 (79, $73,334) and 44108 (62, $50,000). Worth quoting 2–4 unit deals to hold buyers on price per unit and per-foot basis; out-of-state buyers at a $160,000 median are the higher-paying end of the list.

Single-family exits still clear $50/sqft; multifamily exits clear $18

Flippers
170 single-family flips sold at a $68,822 median and $50/sqft, while 33 multifamily flips cleared $50,000 and $18/sqft. The data suggests renovation dollars put into extra footage in 2–4 units are not being paid back at resale. Flip counts sit at 225 versus 388 a year ago, so worth checking your exit comps in the zips that actually printed flips this edition — 44105 (12), 44128 and 44108 (11 each), 44109 and 44110 (11 each).

The per-foot discount is on the hold side, and 767 buyers are already taking it

Landlords
286 two-to-four-unit purchases cleared $103,000 at $52/sqft, and 40 Apartment 5+ trades cleared $340,500 at the same $52/sqft — half the per-foot basis of single family at $108. Since the last drop, 767 local landlords added 1,468 units. Worth checking Cleveland proper, where 1,114 purchases cleared at $105,000 and $68/sqft, against Mentor at $204/sqft and Willoughby at $217/sqft before stretching into the outer ring for the same rent roll.

Small 2–4 unit paper is the gap the recorded market is not filling

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 11 from 64 a year ago at a $60,000 median, while seller-held notes rose to 91 from 72. The largest recorded loans this edition were $327,617 on Brunswick retail and $159,000 on a Fairport Harbor office, with the residential tickets clustered at $93,000–$100,000 on 2–4 units in 44104, 44102 and 44120. The data suggests that band — five-figure loans against $52/sqft multifamily basis — is where borrowers are currently going to seller financing instead.

Retail and industrial per-foot basis sits near multifamily levels

Commercial investors
78 retail/service purchases cleared $314,750 at $78/sqft, 32 offices at $538,750 and $96/sqft, 31 industrial at $375,000 and $50/sqft, and 15 mixed-use at $155,000 and $32/sqft. Only 5 retail flips sold, at a $90,000 median and $18/sqft, so resale liquidity in small commercial is thin. Worth checking the mixed-use and industrial bands where entry per foot is below what single-family buyers are paying, and underwriting to hold rather than to a near-term sale.

Factoids

Numbers from this period a practitioner would repeat to a colleague. Each computed directly from recorded instruments.

2,186 investor purchases — Recorded investor acquisitions across the five-county MSA

holding steady · vs 2,255 a year ago
The buy side is essentially holding, down 3% year over year and up 3% on the prior period. The median purchase slipped to $150,000 from $155,500 a year ago, so volume is stable while basis drifts lower.

$160,000 — Median purchase price paid by out-of-state buyers

trending up · vs $136,750 for local buyers
Out-of-state capital took 31% of investor purchases and paid roughly $23,000 more at the median than locals; California alone accounted for 128 buys, ahead of Florida at 76. Sellers with retail-condition product have a higher-paying channel than the local wholesale bid.

119 buys at a $45,000 median — Investor purchases in 44105 (Slavic Village / Union-Miles)

trending up · vs 93 buys at a $115,000 median in 44102
44105 is the highest-volume zip in the market at the lowest median basis, with 12 flips recorded alongside the buys. Glenville (62 buys, $50,000) and Shaker Square / Buckeye (79 buys, $73,334) fill out the same low-basis band.

91 seller-financed notes — New notes held by investors

trending up · vs 72 a year ago
Seller paper is up 26% year over year at a $138,000 median, versus $122,500 a year ago. Note-holding is absorbing deals that recorded institutional and private debt is not funding.

11 private loans recorded — Recorded private mortgage instruments

trending down · vs 64 a year ago
Recorded private lending is down 83% year over year, with the median loan at $60,000 against $130,418. Some of this is documentation practice rather than dollars, but the recorded channel is thin enough that borrowers are visibly leaning on seller paper.

5.2 months median hold — Time from purchase to flip sale, latest measurable twelve-month cohort

holding steady · middle half held 3.1 to 8.3 months
Of 2,603 measurable flips, 22% turned in under 90 days and 12% took more than a year. Underwriting a six-month hold remains the central case; carry beyond that is a real tail risk in this cohort.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Cleveland-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
