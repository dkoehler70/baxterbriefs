August Edition — Margin Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$57,100 vs $85,000
Median gross margin per flip sold this edition, against the same period a year ago

Median gross flip margin fell to $57,100 from $85,000 a year ago, even as operators cut hold time to 85 days

The 319 flips resold this edition cleared a median $502,900 on a median gross margin of $57,100, down from $85,000 a year ago — 17.3% of sale price versus 25.3%. Exits came faster, not slower: median hold was 85 days against 123, and resales priced at 0.98 of county assessed value versus 1.08. Fewer operators carried the volume, 151 versus 265, with the top ten taking 37.5% of flip revenue against 29.6%. Margins here are gross; rehab and carry are not in the public record.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price to a 17% gross margin, not last year's 25%

Wholesalers
The 21 assignments that surfaced carried a median $25,450 spread, up from $22,498 a year ago, but the buyer's cushion behind them narrowed to 17.3% from 25.3%. It is worth re-underwriting contracts in the mid bands where flip demand still shows up — 80219 (14 flips, $390,000 median buy), 80013 (11 flips, $360,000) and 80015 (8 flips, $365,000) — and expecting more retrades on anything priced off a 2025 comp set. Assignment counts are a floor; only deals that touched a recorded deed appear here.

Speed is doing the work that price used to do

Flippers
Median hold fell to 85 days from 123 and resales cleared at 0.98 of assessed value versus 1.08, with median $/sqft at $317.70 against $329.28. The data suggests the operators still clearing are buying tighter and turning quickly rather than holding for a stretch. Typical exits this period were 1,464 sqft, three-bed, built around 1969, 15.3 km from the CBD — check whether the light-scope version of that box pencils at your cost of capital before you underwrite a full gut.

The cheaper basis is in condos and the outer Aurora zips

Landlords
Investors paid a $295,000 median for condos at $276/sqft against $533,500 and $311/sqft for single family, and the overall investor median fell to $489,000 from $540,000 a year ago. With 93 more homes going into flipper hands than came out this period, some of that inventory will need an exit; buying from operators on day 85 rather than day 30 is worth testing in 80013, 80015 and 80229, where median buys ran $360,000, $365,000 and $282,500.

Recorded loan count halved — shorter paper is the gap

Private lenders
Only 51 private loans were recorded versus 119 a year ago, at a $263,800 median versus $350,000, while seller notes rose to 122 from 87. Trailing-twelve-month median loan-to-price sat at 1.02 with rehab dollars in 53.3% of financed deals, so the volume names — Indicate Capital (49 loans, $505,000 median), Capital Fund I (48, $336,925), RFLF 4 (44, $479,325) — are still writing above purchase price. With median holds at 85 days, short-duration rehab paper on sub-$500,000 basis is the slot the data suggests is thinnest.

2-4 units trade at house-like per-foot pricing

Multifamily investors
Thirty-five 2-4 unit deals recorded at a $604,000 median and $307/sqft — within a few dollars of the $311/sqft single-family figure — while multi-family (unspecified) ran 23 deals at $840,000 and $200/sqft. Small multi remains a rounding error in flip exits at 1.3% of resales versus 0.8% a year ago, so competition from resale operators is limited; the per-foot gap between the 2-4 unit and unspecified multi-family buckets is worth checking parcel by parcel before assuming it is a bargain.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
