August Edition — Inventory Build

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

341 vs 146
Net homes added to flipper-held inventory this edition (662 bought less 321 resold), against a net +146 in the same period a year ago

Flippers bought 662 homes and resold 321, adding a net 341 to inventory against +146 a year ago

Flippers bought 662 homes this edition and resold 321, a net 341 added to inventory versus +146 a year ago. Investor purchases overall ran 1,769 against 1,299 a year ago, at a median $489,000 versus $540,000 — more units at a lower basis. Exits are the constraint: 321 flips sold against 530 a year ago, at a median $502,900 and 0.98 of assessed value, down from 1.08. Recorded counts lag four to eight weeks and the latest month can still grow, but the gap between buying and selling is wide enough to survive backfill.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Spreads held while assignment count thinned

Wholesalers
Only 21 assignments touched a deed this edition against 28 a year ago, but the median spread was $25,450 versus $22,498. With 662 flip acquisitions recorded, end buyers are still active, so the constraint looks like contract supply rather than demand. Worth checking the volume zips where flips still clear — 80219 (27 buys, 14 flips, $390,000 median buy), 80013 (29 buys, 11 flips, $360,000) and 80015 (25 buys, 8 flips, $365,000). Assignment counts are a floor; most never hit the record.

Underwrite the exit, not the entry

Flippers
Buying is outrunning selling by two to one — 662 in, 321 out — and exits now price at 0.98 of assessed against 1.08 a year ago. Median resale was $502,900 at $318.91/sqft, down from $526,000 and $329.57/sqft, on a median 1,464 sqft, 1969-vintage, three-bed house. Median gross margin was $58,000 on an 85-day hold; margins here exclude rehab and carry. The data suggests pricing exits off current $/sqft and assessed-value ratios rather than last year's premium, and holding basis at or below the $489,000 median purchase.

Cheapest recorded entry sits in condo and Aurora product

Landlords
Condos recorded 253 investor buys at a $295,000 median and $276/sqft, against single family at $533,500 and $311/sqft; mobile and manufactured ran 25 deals at $279,700 and $149/sqft. On the map, 80013 ($360,000 median buy) and 80015 ($365,000) sat well under the $489,000 metro median. With flippers adding a net 341 homes to inventory, worth checking aged flip listings in those zips as a source of stabilized-condition acquisitions rather than competing for fresh contracts.

Recorded private paper is half last year's count

Private lenders
51 recorded private loans against 119 a year ago, median $263,800 versus $350,000. Over the trailing 12 months, 71.7% of observed investor purchases were financed, median loan $458,375 at a 0.95 loan-to-price, and 426 loans funded rehab. The rehab-heavy lenders on record — RFLF 4 (44 loans, 86% rehab, 1.13 loan-to-price) and Kiavi (31 loans, 81% rehab, 1.18) — define the competitive terms. With 157 flips aging out of the pipeline in the latest month and inventory at 7,313, extension and takeout requests on stalled projects are the likely deal flow. Terms and rates are not public record.

2-4 unit basis prices above single family per foot

Multifamily investors
35 two-to-four unit purchases recorded at a $604,000 median and $307/sqft, against single family at $311/sqft — no per-foot discount for the extra doors, and the unspecified multi-family line printed $840,000 at $200/sqft across 23 deals. Small multi remains 1.2% of flip exits versus 0.8% a year ago, so competition from resale operators in this product is minimal. With 122 seller-held notes recorded against 87 a year ago, carry-back structures are worth raising directly with owners in this size band.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
