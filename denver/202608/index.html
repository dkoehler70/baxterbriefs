August Edition — Margin Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$57,550 vs $83,150
Median gross margin per recorded flip this edition, against the same period a year ago

Flip margins compressed to $57,550 while holds shortened to 85 days, and the flipper bench halved

Flips recorded this edition sold at a median gross margin of $57,550, or 17.5% of sale price, against $83,150 and 22.9% a year ago. Holds compressed at the same time: a median 85 days from purchase to resale versus 130 days, with 51% of exits under 90 days. The bench thinned with it — 152 distinct flippers sold this period against 292 a year ago, and the ten busiest took 37.4% of exits versus 28.0%. Median resale came in at 0.98 of assessed value, down from 1.08.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price to the 17.5% margin, not last year's 22.9%

Wholesalers
Median assignment spread surfaced at $25,450 versus $22,498 a year ago, on 21 recorded assignments (a floor). With buyer margins at a median $57,550, contracts priced off year-ago comps will sit. The data suggests the deepest flip demand is in 80219 (14 flips against 27 investor buys), 80013 (11 of 29) and 80011 (11 of 16) — all median buys between $339,950 and $390,000.

Buy where the recorded buy-to-sale gap is still wide

Flippers
Zips where median investor buy sits well under median flip resale this period are worth checking: 80020 Broomfield ($415,000 buy vs $606,450 sale), 80127 Ken Caryl ($568,350 vs $750,000), 80237 Denver Tech Center ($350,000 vs $516,250) and 80229 Thornton ($282,500 vs $372,500). Two zips inverted — 80231 Hampden ($268,700 buy vs $247,500 sale) and 80210 Platt Park ($840,000 vs $775,000) — and deserve tighter underwriting.

Condo and Aurora basis are the cheapest entries in the file

Landlords
Condos bought at a $295,000 median and $276/sqft against single family at $533,500 and $311/sqft; Aurora's citywide $239/sqft compares to Denver's $328/sqft on 234 purchases. Mobile/manufactured came in lowest at $149/sqft on 25 buys. With flip exits thinning, hold-focused buyers are competing against fewer rehab bidders in the sub-$300,000 condo band.

Fill the 85-day bridge that banks are not pricing

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 51 from 119 a year ago while seller-financed notes rose to 122 from 87 — sellers are filling the gap. Median hold on completed flips is 85 days with 51% under 90, so short-dated paper is what borrowers actually need. Rehab-heavy lenders are already going above purchase price (Merchants at 1.07 loan-to-price, RFLF 4 at 1.13, Kiavi at 1.21) versus a 0.95 median across all financed purchases.

2-4 units trade at a third of the per-foot cost of 5+

Multifamily investors
35 two-to-four unit purchases recorded at a $604,000 median and $307/sqft, against 10 apartment 5+ deals at a $3.17M median and $461/sqft; multi-family unspecified sat lowest at $200/sqft on 23 buys. Small multi remains almost absent from flip exits — 2 of 320, or 1.25% versus 0.77% a year ago — so resale comps are thin and the data suggests underwriting to hold, not resale.

trending down · vs 119 a year ago at a $350,000 median

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
