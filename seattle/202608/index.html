August Edition — Thinner Bench

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

101 vs 199
Distinct operators recording a flip resale this edition, versus the same two months a year ago

The flipper roster halved: 101 operators recorded a resale, against 199 a year ago

One hundred one operators recorded a flip resale this edition, against 199 a year ago, and the deal count fell with them: 125 resales versus 243. The buy side is contracting faster than the sell side, with flippers taking title to 191 homes against 376 a year ago, so the pipeline refilling behind these exits is thin. What remains is more concentrated: the top ten operators by resale revenue took 37.7% of the dollars, up from 32.6%. Counts are recorded instruments lagging four to eight weeks, and the most recent month can still grow.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer end buyers means shorter buyer lists and tighter spreads

Wholesalers
Flipper acquisitions ran 191 this edition against 376 a year ago, and only 101 operators recorded a resale, so assignment demand is thinner than the 2 surfaced assignments alone suggest (wholesale counts are a floor). The data suggests concentrating on where the 4-8 lifetime-flip band actually works: Lakewood West 98498, Spanaway South 98387, Parkland 98444, East Tacoma 98404 and Bonney Lake 98391. Bring contracts priced against a $409/sqft exit, not last year's $446.

Buy to a 226-day hold, not a 204-day one

Flippers
Median gross margin came in at 52.8% versus 59.0% a year ago on a 226-day median hold, so carry assumptions built last summer are stale. The typical resale this edition was 1,691 sqft, 3 bed, built 1968, about 34 km from the Seattle CBD, and 25.6% were pre-1950 against 23.0% a year ago. Pierce took 36.8% of flip resales versus 33.3% a year ago while King slipped to 41.6% from 45.7%; worth checking whether your acquisition footprint has drifted with it.

The per-foot gap favours 2-4 units and manufactured product

Landlords
Investors paid a median $314/sqft across 84 two-to-four unit purchases and $220/sqft across 59 mobile or manufactured deals, against $451/sqft on 1,679 single-family buys. With overall investor purchase medians at $715,000 versus $750,000 a year ago, entry pricing is modestly softer market-wide. Lower-basis buy zips worth screening include Lake Stevens 98258 at a $620,000 median buy and Everett South 98203 at $480,000.

Fill the extension and payoff gap the slower exits create

Private lenders
Only 78 private loans were recorded this edition against 101 a year ago, while trailing-twelve-month bank and fund lending shows a median loan of $475,075 at a 0.99 loan-to-price and 47.0% of 953 loans carrying rehab funds. Eastside Funding recorded 60 loans and Kiavi 59 over that window. With holds at 226 days and resales at 125 versus 243, the data suggests demand for extension and takeout paper on aged projects rather than new-purchase originations. Interest rates and terms are not recorded and are not published here.

Small-scale residential trades are where the volume is

Multifamily investors
Apartment 5+ recorded 16 purchases at a $2,299,000 median and unspecified multi-family 8 at $2,117,400, against 84 two-to-four unit deals at $727,500 and $314/sqft. Small multifamily made up 0.8% of flip resales this edition, down from 1.6% a year ago, so competition from renovate-and-sell operators on that product is limited. With 180 seller notes recorded versus 154 a year ago, seller paper is worth raising in negotiations on 2-4 unit stock.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
