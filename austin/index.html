August Edition — Exit Concentration

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

92.8% vs 37.6%
Share of recorded flip resales sold by the ten most active flippers this edition, against the same share a year ago

The flip resale market has narrowed to a handful of sellers: the top 10 accounted for 92.8% of recorded flip exits versus 37.6% a year ago, and the exits moved north into Williamson County

Fifty-six flips were recorded this edition against 82 a year ago, but the seller list thinned faster than the count: 38 distinct flippers sold, down from 62, and the top ten took 92.8% of the exits versus 37.6% a year ago. The geography moved with them. Williamson County took 30.4% of flip resales, up from 17.1%, while Travis fell to 51.8% from 65.9%, and the median resale sat 26.2 km from the core versus 19.4 km. Retail exit competition inside Travis is currently thin and concentrated in a few names.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Fewer recorded assignments, and the buy side is a short list

Wholesalers
Only 12 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds against 28 a year ago, and that count is a floor. With 38 flippers accounting for this edition's exits and names like Brookfield (37 flips) and Millrose in Elgin (10) doing repeat volume, the data suggests building a buyer list around the concentrated Williamson and eastern corridor names rather than broadcasting. Sub-$215,000 median product in Dale ($185,069), Kyle ($188,700) and Elgin ($205,332) is where buy-box depth still shows.

Retail exit competition in Travis is thinner than it looks

Flippers
Travis took 51.8% of flip resales this edition versus 65.9% a year ago, and Williamson rose to 30.4% from 17.1%. Georgetown West (78628) logged 4 flips on 33 investor buys at a $379,050 median buy; 78745 logged 3 flips on 37 buys. Percentage margin at 24.7% versus 18.2% argues for the lower-basis product; median hold of 171 days argues for pricing six months of carry into the offer.

Basis is cheaper per foot outside the core

Landlords
Median investor purchase fell to $357,890 from $399,000 a year ago, and the per-foot gap is wide: Liberty Hill $147/sqft, Kyle $150/sqft, Hutto $154/sqft against Austin at $349/sqft. With 1,415 local landlords adding 2,823 units since the last drop, competition for that outer-ring product is real. Worth checking rent-to-basis on the Kyle and Hutto medians ($188,700 and $327,559) before paying Austin per-foot pricing.

The gap sits with the 3-to-8-flip operators

Private lenders
Recorded private loans ran 30 at a $260,900 median against 156 a year ago, while 155 seller-financed notes were recorded at a $279,000 median. The 565 investors in the 3-to-8 lifetime flip band show a 193-day median hold and a $90,440 median margin, which is the profile that a 6-to-9-month bridge fits. Note that 28% of the 8,559 flippers on record are lifetime net-negative, so sponsor track record matters more than the pro forma.

Small multifamily is priced below houses per foot

Multifamily investors
32 recorded 2-4 unit buys at a $446,134 median and $214/sqft compare with 505 single-family buys at $251/sqft. Thirteen larger residential multifamily trades cleared at a $14.1M median and $157/sqft, and the largest recorded purchases sat in Leander, Round Rock, Kyle and Georgetown rather than central Austin. The data suggests the per-foot discount is in the small-unit-count product, where houses set the comp.

Flip Pipeline — Buying, Selling and Inventory

Homes bought by known flippers and homes they resold, per month, alongside the stock sitting in flipper hands. A house enters inventory when a flipper buys it and leaves on the next recorded sale — at any price, on any timeline, which is a wider net than the flip count elsewhere in this brief. A purchase stops counting after five years unsold, on the view that by then it is a hold rather than a flip in progress.

Solid = recorded, dashed = projected. Green bars are inventory — homes a flipper has bought and not yet resold — read against the RIGHT axis, which is which starts at zero so the bar heights can be compared directly. Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
