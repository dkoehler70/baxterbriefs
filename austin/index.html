August Edition — Basis Split

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$140/sqft vs $251/sqft
Median price per square foot on flipped single-family resales this edition, against the median per-square-foot investors paid for single-family purchases in the same period.

Flip execution has moved out of the zips where investor dollars land: flipped single-family resold at $140/sqft while investors bought single-family at $251/sqft

Flip execution and acquisition capital are no longer in the same product. The 106 single-family flips that closed this edition resold at a median $140/sqft, while investors paid a median $251/sqft for the single-family they bought. The split is geographic: Austin absorbed 357 purchases at a $720,000 median and $359/sqft with almost no flip activity, while flips cluster in the low-basis ring — Kyle's 78640 logged 8 flips against a $276,806 median buy. Renovation margin now sits outside the zips where most investor dollars are being deployed.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Source where flips actually clear, not where buy counts are highest

Wholesalers
Only 13 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds versus 34 a year ago, and that count is a floor. The zips still absorbing renovation product are 78640 Kyle (8 flips, $276,806 median buy), 78628 Georgetown West (8 flips), 78745 (6 flips) and 78634 Hutto (5 flips). Bastrop (48 buys, 3 flips), Dale (35 buys, 0 flips) and Elgin (34 buys, 1 flip) are heavy on land and landlord buyers, so it is worth checking whether your contract list there matches a buyer who never renovates.

Underwrite to a $140/sqft exit, and buy basis below it

Flippers
Single-family flips resold at a median $322,529 and $140/sqft this edition. That math does not work against Austin acquisition basis at $359/sqft; it does work in Kyle ($155/sqft, $276,972 median), Hutto ($158/sqft), Elgin ($165/sqft) and Round Rock ($189/sqft). The data suggests holding the buy plus rehab under the $315,542 flip median and planning on roughly a 179-day hold rather than a fast turn.

The low-basis ring is where cost per square foot still favors holders

Landlords
Jarrell ($119/sqft), Kyle ($155/sqft) and Hutto ($158/sqft) are the cheapest square footage in the top-15 city table, and single-family overall traded at $419,415 and $251/sqft. Condos (86 purchases, $297,150 median) and 2-4 units (32 at $446,134, $214/sqft) offer lower entry tickets. With flip supply down to 114 sold, fewer renovated houses are being pushed into the resale channel — worth checking whether tired inventory in those zips is now cheaper to buy than to bid on renovated.

Fill the sub-$350,000 rehab gap, but price to the exit, not the ARV pitch

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 36 from 180 a year ago while the median loan rose to $260,900, leaving small suburban rehab deals thinly financed. Seller financing partly filled it: 186 notes at a $282,175 median, up from $200,000 a year ago. The lifetime scorecard argues for discipline — median profit is $6,500 per flip and 28% of the 8,559 local flippers are net-negative — so sizing against a $140/sqft resale rather than an optimistic ARV is the defensible approach.

Small retail is the liquid line; verify recorded values before comping

Commercial investors
Retail and service recorded 55 purchases at a $1.33M median ($427/sqft), against 12 industrial at $2.91M and 6 office at $1.93M ($431/sqft) — retail is where deal count actually exists at a small-investor ticket. Note that 32 recorded values were excluded as implausible, including portfolio prices stamped per parcel, so single-comp underwriting off recorded amounts is risky. Counts here lag 4-8 weeks and the most recent month can grow up to roughly 15%.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
