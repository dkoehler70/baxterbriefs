August Edition — Margin Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$315,542 vs $359,100
Median flip resale price this edition against the same period a year ago, while the median investor purchase was $408,690 vs $406,381 a year ago

Flip resale medians fell 12% year over year to $315,542 while the median investor purchase held flat at $408,690 — the adjustment is landing entirely on the exit side

Flips sold this edition cleared a median $315,542, down from $359,100 a year ago, while the median investor purchase held at $408,690 against $406,381 a year ago. Volume moved with price: 114 flip resales versus 187 a year ago. Acquisition pricing has not repriced to the weaker exit, and the lifetime record for the 8,559 flippers on file — median profit $6,500 per flip, 28% of flippers net-negative — leaves thin room to absorb a softer resale. Counts lag 4-8 weeks and the most recent month can grow up to about 15% as late records arrive.

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price to landlord math, not flipper ARV, in the cheap outer ring

Wholesalers
Only 13 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds versus 34 a year ago, and that is a floor. With flip buyers down to 114 from 187, the reliable end buyer this period is the landlord: Bastrop (49 buys, 3 flips, $200,032 median), Dale (35 buys, 0 flips, $185,069) and Elgin (34 buys, 1 flip, $218,942). It is worth testing spreads underwritten to rent yield in the $185,000-$220,000 band rather than to a resale comp.

Re-cut acquisition offers against a $315,542 exit, not last year's $359,100

Flippers
The data suggests the buy side has not adjusted: investor purchase medians are flat at $408,690 while resale medians dropped 12%. Single-family flips cleared at $140/sqft. The zips still absorbing flip product are 78640 Kyle and 78628 Georgetown West at 8 each, then 78745 at 6 and 78641 and 78634 at 5. Underwriting carry to the 5.9-month median hold — with a 9.0-month upper quartile — is worth doing before committing.

The accumulation is happening outside the core

Landlords
1,415 landlords added 2,823 units since the last drop, and 2,457 first-time local landlords appeared in the records. Basis, not appreciation, is doing the work: Kyle $155/sqft, Hutto $158/sqft, Elgin $165/sqft against Austin at $359/sqft. Condos at 86 purchases and a $297,150 median, plus 2-4 unit at $214/sqft, are worth screening against the $251/sqft single-family median.

Short bridge on sub-$350,000 rehab exits is the thin spot

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 36 from 180 a year ago at a $260,900 median, while seller notes at 188 carry a $284,071 median, up from $200,000. That leaves the small-balance rehab bridge underserved just as flip exits cleared at $315,542. Structuring to the 5.9-month median hold with a defined extension, and pricing the 13% tail that runs past a year, matches what the hold data shows.

Retail is the priced product; small multifamily is the discount

Commercial investors
Retail/service logged 55 purchases at a $1.33M median and $427/sqft, and office 6 at $431/sqft — both above the $251/sqft single-family median. By contrast, 2-4 unit product traded 32 times at $446,134 and $214/sqft. Industrial showed 12 purchases at a $2.91M median and $201/sqft. For anyone stepping up from houses, the 2-4 unit and industrial bands are where the per-foot basis is lowest this period.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
