August Edition — Flip Contraction

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

132 vs 230
Flips sold this edition versus the same two months a year ago, against a 1,219 vs 1,411 decline in overall investor purchases

Flip resales are contracting twice as fast as investor buying, and at a lower price point

Flip resales came in at 132 this edition against 230 a year ago, a far steeper fall than the 1,219 vs 1,411 decline in investor purchases overall. The median flip resale slipped to $330,106 from $406,442 a year ago and $367,332 in the prior period. Single-family flips cleared at a median $144/sqft, while investor single-family purchases ran $248/sqft, so the exit channel is skewing to cheaper, smaller product than the acquisition pool. Lifetime records on the 8,559 flippers here show a median $6,500 profit per flip and 28% net-negative, leaving little cushion at this spread.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price to a landlord exit, not a flip exit

Wholesalers
With 14 recorded assignments against 47 a year ago and flip resales at 132 vs 230, the flipper buyer pool is thinner. The data suggests working the low-basis outer zips where flips are near zero but buying is active — Bastrop 78602 (51 buys, 3 flips, median $201,362), Elgin 78621 (35 buys, 1 flip, $221,892) and Dale 78616 (35 buys, 0 flips, $185,069) — and marketing to hold buyers rather than rehab crews.

Underwrite to $330,106 and a nine-month hold

Flippers
Median flip resale is $330,106, down from $367,332 in the prior period and $406,442 a year ago, while median investor acquisition price sits flat at $418,950. Latest measurable hold time is 5.8 months with the middle half at 3.5–8.9 months and 13% past a year, so carry assumptions built on a 90-day turn are worth revisiting. Zips still clearing volume are worth checking first: Kyle 78640 (9 flips), Georgetown West 78628 (8), South Austin 78745 (6).

Basis, not price, is where the outer zips pay

Landlords
Median purchase price is unchanged at $418,950, but $/sqft varies widely: Hutto $158, Kyle $160, Elgin $165 against Austin at $367. With 1,415 landlords adding 2,823 units and flip exits shrinking, buying finished rehab product from flippers carrying past the 5.8-month median hold is worth testing. Condos, 92 buys at a median $308,910, and 2-4 units, 33 at $453,369 ($210/sqft), are the two smaller-ticket pockets in the data.

Fill the gap the 40 recorded loans left

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 40 from 185 a year ago while seller-financed notes held at 207 with a median of $285,000, up from $202,410 — borrowers are finding paper, just not from recorded lenders. The data suggests demand for bridge terms sized near the $260,900 median loan and structured to the 5.8-month median hold, with extension pricing for the 13% of flips that run past a year. Note that 28% of flippers on record are lifetime net-negative.

Retail and small multifamily carry the non-residential flow

Commercial investors
Retail/service ran 71 purchases at a median $1.32M ($427/sqft), industrial 18 at $6.25M and unspecified multifamily 16 at $9.60M, with the largest recorded deals in Austin industrial. Residential lots totaled 230 buys at $317,836 while new-build sales fell to 106 from 126 a year ago, so lot absorption is worth watching before taking down land. The 2-4 unit segment, 33 deals at $453,369, sits between the residential and commercial books.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
