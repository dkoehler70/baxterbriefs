August Edition — Flip Pipeline

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

210 bought vs 54 sold
Homes acquired by flippers versus flips resold this edition, against 180 bought and 73 sold a year ago

Flippers are stacking inventory faster than they clear it: 210 homes bought against 54 resales, with the median flip margin down to $85,940 from $121,000

Flippers recorded 210 acquisitions this edition against 54 resales, versus 180 bought and 73 sold a year ago — a net add of 156 homes to the pipeline against 107 last year. The exits that did clear moved faster, at a median 165-day hold versus 243, but the median gross margin fell to $85,940 from $121,000. The sell side is also narrower: 37 distinct flipper-sellers versus 61, with the top ten carrying 93% of resale volume versus 35%. Recorded counts lag four to eight weeks, and the most recent month can grow up to about 15%.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Feed the acquisition side, not the exit side

Wholesalers
Flippers bought 210 homes this edition against 54 resales, so demand is on intake. Recorded assignments were 12 versus 28 a year ago — a floor, not a ceiling. The data suggests the sub-$210,000 zips are where buy boxes are open: Bastrop 78602 (38 buys, $196,540 median), Kyle 78640 (31 buys, $188,700), Elgin 78621 (30 buys, $205,332) and Dale 78616 (27 buys, $185,069). Worth checking whether your buyer list still skews Travis when half of flip exits no longer do.

Underwrite to a $85,940 gross margin and a 165-day hold

Flippers
Median hold on cleared flips was 165 days, with the middle half between 90 and 256 days and 9% over a year. Median margin was $85,940 against $121,000 a year ago on a $453,297 median sale. Two zips are worth re-checking before committing: Round Rock 78664 shows a $266,000 median flip sale against a $299,294 median buy, and Georgetown West 78628 shows $388,857 against $379,050. Thin or inverted spreads at the zip median argue for tighter product selection, not more volume.

Buy the per-foot gap while flippers compete on the same houses

Landlords
Austin purchases ran a $349/sqft median against Liberty Hill $147, Kyle $150, Hutto $154, Georgetown $170 and San Marcos $170. Mobile/manufactured product came in at $188,390 median ($142/sqft) across 30 purchases. With 1,415 local landlords adding 2,823 units since the last data drop, competition is real, but the basis spread suggests outlying product still pencils differently than central Travis. Rent support, not headline basis, is the variable to verify per zip.

Bridge the gap between 210 acquisitions and 54 exits

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 30 (median $260,900) from 156 a year ago, while flipper acquisitions rose to 210 and flip inventory stands at 2,868 homes. That combination points to demand for short-term paper that is not currently recorded as private lending. Trailing-year purchases show a 1.02 median loan-to-price and a $345,000 median loan — thin cushion at origination. The data suggests pricing to a 165-day median hold with real extension terms, and treating the 9% of flips that ran past a year as the tail risk.

Small multi is trading cheaper per foot than houses

Multifamily investors
Thirty-two 2-4 unit purchases cleared at a $446,134 median and $214/sqft, below the $251/sqft on single family. Small multi also fell to 5.6% of flips from 8.2% a year ago, so fewer renovation buyers are bidding against you on duplex and fourplex stock. Worth checking Round Rock ($184/sqft citywide) and Georgetown ($170/sqft), where investor volume is heavy but flip exits there are already showing flat-to-negative zip-level spreads.

Factoids

Numbers from this period a practitioner would repeat to a colleague. Each computed directly from recorded instruments.

trending up · vs 35% a year ago
Only 37 distinct sellers recorded a flip this edition, down from 61. The resale side of the market is now dominated by a handful of programmatic operators, which makes comp-setting in a given zip more sensitive to one seller's pricing.

31.5% of flip exits in Williamson — Williamson County share of recorded flip resales

trending up · vs 16.4% a year ago
Travis fell to 50% from 64% over the same span, and the median flip sat 27.8 km from the CBD versus 19.0 km. Exit comps built on central-Travis experience will not travel to Georgetown, Round Rock or Jarrell product.

30 private loans recorded — Recorded private-lender loans, median $260,900

trending down · vs 156 a year ago at a $202,500 median
Fewer, larger recorded loans. With 28% of trailing-year purchases showing recorded financing (364 of 1,313, median loan $345,000), most acquisition capital in this data is not surfacing as a recorded private note.

155 seller-financed notes at $279,000 — New seller-held notes and their median size

trending down · vs 239 notes at a $191,000 median a year ago
Note count is down 35% year over year while the typical note is larger. Seller paper is migrating from low-basis outlying product toward mid-priced houses, which changes who a note buyer is competing with.

17% of investor buys from out of state — Out-of-state share of 882 investor purchases, median $370,431

holding steady · vs 66% local at a $347,096 median
California alone accounted for 50 purchases, ahead of Utah at 12 and New York and Kansas at 8 each. Out-of-state buyers are paying above the local median, which matters most in the zips where they cluster.

2-4 units at $214/sqft — Median price per square foot on 32 small-multi purchases, median price $446,134

trending down · vs $251/sqft on 505 single-family purchases
Small multi is trading below detached houses on a per-foot basis this edition, and mobile/manufactured lower still at $142/sqft on 30 purchases. The basis gap is the cleanest argument for product substitution at a given budget.

$6,500 median profit per flip — Lifetime median profit across 8,559 recorded local flippers, 25,036 flips

holding steady · with 28% of flippers net-negative lifetime
The long-run distribution is thin even in good years. Underwriting that assumes the median operator earns the current $85,940 gross margin ignores carry, rehab and the quarter of flippers who never got there.

Flip Pipeline — Buying, Selling and Inventory

Homes bought by known flippers and homes they resold, per month, alongside the stock sitting in flipper hands. A house enters inventory when a flipper buys it and leaves on the next recorded sale — at any price, on any timeline, which is a wider net than the flip count elsewhere in this brief. A purchase stops counting after five years unsold, on the view that by then it is a hold rather than a flip in progress.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
