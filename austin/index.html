August Edition — Flip Map Shift

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

46% vs 60%
Travis County's share of flips sold this edition, against its share a year ago

Flip exits moved out of Travis County: 46% of resales cleared there this edition versus 60% a year ago, with Williamson at 31% and the median flip 27.6 km from downtown

Travis County took 46% of flip resales this edition, down from 60% a year ago, while Williamson rose to 31% from 21% and Caldwell to 8% from 2%. The median flip sold 27.6 km from downtown, against 22.8 km a year ago. Exit pricing followed the map: the median flip cleared $480,861 versus $644,144, at $307 per square foot versus $332. Georgetown West (78628) went from none of the year-ago flips to 10% of this edition's, and Round Rock (78664) from none to 8%.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Contract where the buy side actually is: Kyle, Bastrop, Elgin

Wholesalers
Only 13 assignments surfaced on deeds this edition versus 34 a year ago, and the surviving demand is priced low. Kyle (78640) took 46 investor buys at a $276,806 median, Bastrop (78602) 48 at $200,032, and Elgin (78621) 34 at $218,942. Worth checking whether your buyer list still leans Travis; the flip exits there fell to 46% of the market from 60%.

Underwrite exits at $307/sqft, not last year's $332

Flippers
The median flip cleared $480,861 this edition against $644,144 a year ago, and price per square foot slid to $307 from $332. Where resales are actually clearing has shifted: Georgetown West (78628) and Round Rock (78664) each went from no share of year-ago flips to 10% and 8%. The data suggests spec budgets built on Travis-level exit pricing need a second look before contract.

The $185k–$220k band outside the core still exists

Landlords
Dale posted 36 investor buys at a $185,069 median, Maxwell 29 at $219,450, and Bastrop 48 at $200,032 — all well under the $395,641 metro investor median. With 1,415 landlords adding 2,823 units since the last drop, competition for these is other small operators rather than funds. Rent-to-basis math, not appreciation, is what these zips are being bought on.

Fill the gap the 36-loan quarter left

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 36 from 185 a year ago and seller-financed notes to 187 from 279, so borrowers on outer-ring rehabs have fewer recorded options. The median recorded loan is $260,900, which brackets the Round Rock, Hutto and Kyle purchase bands. Sponsor screening matters here: across 8,559 local flippers on record, median lifetime profit is $6,500 per flip and 28% are net-negative.

Retail/service is the only mid-size category with depth

Commercial investors
Retail and service recorded 55 investor purchases at a $1.33M median ($427/sqft), against 12 industrial at $2.91M and 13 multi-family at $14.1M. Small-balance 2–4 unit deals numbered 32 at a $446,135 median. Note that 32 recorded values were excluded as implausible portfolio stamps, so verify any headline price against the parcel before using it as a comp.

Two hundred purchases came from outside Texas, led by California at 59 and New York at 31. Out-of-state capital is transacting roughly $86,000 above the local median ticket, which shows up most in land and higher-basis zips.

95.2% vs 50.8% — Share of flip resale dollars taken by the ten largest flippers

trending up · vs 50.8% a year ago
Concentration among flip sellers jumped, with the Herfindahl index at 7,642 against 428 a year ago. A shrinking number of shops is clearing the exits, so comparable-sale reads on a given street may now reflect one operator's pricing rather than a market.

36 private loans — Recorded private loans this edition, median $260,900

trending down · vs 185 a year ago, median $205,000
Recorded private lending is down 81% year over year even as ticket size rose. Fewer, larger recorded notes means renovation capital is either coming from unrecorded sources or from balance sheets.

88 new-build sales — Builder new-build closings, median $459,832

trending down · vs 120 a year ago, median $464,170
Builder volume fell 27% year over year while the median price barely moved. Less new supply in the outer ring is relevant to flippers and landlords who compete with incentive-laden builder inventory on resale.

$155/sqft in Kyle — Median investor purchase price per square foot, Kyle (45 buys)

trending down · vs $359/sqft in Austin (357 buys)
Hutto at $158/sqft and Round Rock at $189/sqft sit in the same band. The basis gap to Austin is the mechanical reason flip and rental acquisition keeps drifting to the Williamson and Hays edges.

13 assignments — Wholesale assignments surfacing on recorded deeds, median spread $409,220

trending down · vs 34 a year ago, median spread $496,156
Recorded assignments are a floor, since most never touch a deed, but the direction matches the 38% drop from the prior period. Fewer surfaced spreads alongside a lower median suggests thinner contract pricing at the top end.

1,415 landlords added units — Local landlords that grew a portfolio since the last data drop, +2,823 units

trending up · alongside 2,457 first-time local landlords appearing in the records
Buy-and-hold formation continued even as total investor purchases fell 13% year over year. Small landlords, not funds, absorbed most of the outer-ring product this edition.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
