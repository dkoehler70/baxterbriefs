August Edition — Flip Basis

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$140 vs $208/sqft
Median price per square foot on flips resold this edition, against flips resold a year ago

Flips are clearing at $140 per square foot, down from $208 a year ago — same size house, weaker exit

Flips sold this edition cleared a median $140/sqft, against $208/sqft a year ago, on essentially the same house — median 1,828 sqft versus 1,792. Volume fell alongside price: 114 flips versus 187 a year ago, at a median $315,542 versus $359,100. Exits are also printing below the assessor's mark, with the median sale at 0.87 times assessed value versus 1.09 a year ago. The map moved with the money: Travis County took 47% of flips versus 59% a year ago, while Williamson rose to 33% from 19%.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price to the $140/sqft exit, not last year's comps

Wholesalers
With flip resales clearing a median $140/sqft and 114 flips versus 187 a year ago, the buyer list for retail-grade rehab paper is shorter. The data suggests the more liquid assignments sit in the low-basis outer zips — Dale at a $185,069 median buy, Bastrop $200,032, Elgin $218,942 — where landlord buyers, not flippers, take the contract. Only 13 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds versus 34 a year ago, and that count is a floor.

Underwrite ARV against recorded resales at 0.87x assessed

Flippers
Flips resold this edition ran a median 0.87 times assessed value, against 1.09 a year ago; anchoring ARV to the assessor or to 2025 comps overstates the exit. Worth checking where exits still happen: Kyle (78640, 8 flips on 46 buys) and Georgetown West (78628, 8 flips on 44 buys). Hold-time baseline for measurable flips was a median 5.9 months, with the middle half at 3.6–9.0 months — carry cost should be underwritten to the long end, and note 28% of the 8,559 flippers on record are lifetime net-negative.

The $/sqft gap between the core and the ring is the whole trade

Landlords
Median investor purchase $/sqft: Austin $359, versus Jarrell $119, Kyle $155, Hutto $158, Elgin $165. With 1,415 local landlords adding 2,823 units since the last drop, competition for the ring product is real but the basis spread remains the clearest edge in the data. Condos are a second lane — 86 purchases at a $297,150 median, $250/sqft, near single-family's $251/sqft on smaller tickets.

Fill the gap flippers can't clear at today's exits

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 36 from 180 a year ago while seller-financed notes ran 186 at a $282,175 median — sellers, not lenders, are carrying paper. That leaves room at the $260,900 median loan size, but exits printing at 0.87x assessed argue for sizing to a recorded-resale ARV rather than assessed or list. The active lender bench is thin: the busiest, Road Less Taken LLC, shows 5 loans over trailing twelve months.

Small multifamily is the cheapest square footage on the board

Commercial investors
2-4 unit purchases — 32 deals at a $446,135 median, $214/sqft — price below single family's $251/sqft and far below retail/service at $427/sqft on 55 deals ($1.33M median). Industrial saw 12 purchases at a $2.91M median, $201/sqft. For investors sizing the step up from houses, the small-multi band is where the recorded basis is lowest relative to product type this edition.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
