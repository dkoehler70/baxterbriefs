August Edition — Flip Basis Gap

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$144/sqft vs $251/sqft
Median price per square foot on single-family flip resales this edition, against the median investor single-family purchase

Flip resales are clearing at $144/sqft while investors are buying single-family at $251/sqft, and the gap is geographic

The 107 single-family flips that resold this edition cleared at a median $144 per square foot, while investors bought single-family at a median $251 per square foot. The spread is mostly geography, not margin collapse: flips concentrated in Kyle (9), Georgetown West (8) and South Austin (6), and suburban basis runs $160/sqft in Kyle and $158/sqft in Hutto against $367/sqft across 373 Austin purchases. Median flip resale was $315,748 versus a $426,894 median single-family acquisition. On these numbers the renovation-exit business is working in the ring, not in the core.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price contracts to a $144/sqft exit, not to core Austin comps

Wholesalers
Only 13 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds versus 34 a year ago, and that count is a floor. The data suggests the deepest buy-side demand sits in the sub-$250k periphery: Bastrop 51 buys at a $201,362 median, Elgin 35 at $221,892, Dale 35 at $185,069. Worth checking whether your spread survives when the end buyer underwrites resale near $144/sqft rather than the $251/sqft acquisition median.

Buy where flips are actually reselling: Kyle, Georgetown West, South Austin

Flippers
115 flips closed at a $315,748 median, with single-family at $327,712 and $144/sqft. Flip counts cluster in 78640 (9), 78628 (8) and 78745 (6), while Austin buys carry a $367/sqft basis that leaves no room at current exit pricing. With a 5.9-month median hold and 13% of flips taking over a year, it is worth carrying nine months of interest in the model before signing.

The $158 to $191 per foot suburban belt is where basis still works

Landlords
Hutto printed 32 buys at $334,076 and $158/sqft, Kyle 47 at $278,811 and $160/sqft, Pflugerville 29 at $345,786 and $191/sqft, Round Rock 68 at $356,550 and $190/sqft. Small multifamily remains thin at 32 two-to-four unit purchases ($446,134, $214/sqft). With 1,415 local landlords adding 2,823 units this drop, competition for the cheap end is real; the data suggests bidding discipline matters more than speed.

Fill the gap seller paper is leaving on renovation deals

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 36 from 180 a year ago at a $260,900 median, while 190 seller-financed notes were written at $284,821. That leaves rehab borrowers in the $250k to $350k suburban band underserved. Given the 5.9-month median hold and a 3.6-to-9.0-month middle half, terms sized to nine or twelve months with extension pricing look better matched to how this cohort actually exits.

Small retail is the only liquid commercial line; screen out the mega-prints

Commercial investors
Retail and service recorded 58 purchases at a $1,435,568 median and $440/sqft, against 18 industrial at $6.2M and only 6 office at $1.9M. Headline totals are distorted by a handful of very large deals, including a $332.5M industrial block in Austin, so median rather than total is the usable figure. Worth checking retail and other land (133 buys at $485,518) in the Georgetown and Round Rock corridors where residential investors are already concentrated.

Out-of-area capital is buying a more expensive product than the local book, with CA (59) and NY (31) the top feeders. Local operators are competing hardest at the middle of the stack, not the top.

36 private loans — Recorded private loans this edition, median $260,900

trending down · vs 180 a year ago
Recorded hard-money volume is down 80% year over year while the median loan size rose from $222,350. Fewer, larger loans mean rehab crews are either self-funding or using unrecorded facilities.

190 seller-financed notes at $284,821 — New notes held, count down but median up sharply

trending down · vs 263 notes at a $200,000 median a year ago
Seller paper is thinner but is being written on much higher-priced collateral than a year ago. It is now the largest recorded source of non-bank credit in the metro, at more than five times the private-loan count.

217 residential lot purchases — Vacant residential land buys at a $304,443 median

trending up · vs 483 single-family purchases at $426,894
Dirt is now roughly two lots for every four houses in the recorded investor book, at a materially lower ticket. Land competition in Georgetown, Elgin and Maxwell is coming from builders and out-of-state funds rather than retail rehabbers.

5.9 months median hold — Hold period on flips sold in the latest fully measurable twelve months (1,612 of 1,713 measured)

holding steady · middle half 3.6 to 9.0 months; 13% took over a year
Only 19% turned in under 90 days, so carry cost belongs in every underwrite at six months minimum. Lifetime records for 8,559 local flippers show a $6,500 median profit per flip and 28% of flippers net-negative.

46 buys at a $3.07M median in 78752 — Highland's headline zip median is a recording artifact, not a market price

holding steady · vs $473,480 median buy in 78745 and $277,892 in 78640
Karnes County National Bank alone recorded 39 deals at a $3,070,000 median in Austin, which drives the Highland figure. Screen zip medians for single-name bank and builder blocks before using them as comps.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Same-window, same-source
"Vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
