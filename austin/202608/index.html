August Edition — Outward Drift

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

27.8 km vs 19.0 km
Median distance from downtown Austin of flips resold this edition, against flips resold a year ago

Flip exits have drifted out of the urban core: the median flip resold this period sat 27.8 km from downtown, against 19.0 km a year ago, as Williamson County's share of exits nearly doubled

The flip map has moved outward. Travis County took 50% of flip exits this edition against 64% a year ago, while Williamson rose to 31% from 16%; Georgetown West went from no exits a year ago to 7.4% of them, and Round Rock from 1.4% to 5.6%. The price effect follows the geography: median flip resale of $453,297 against $538,000 a year ago, and $256/sqft against $292/sqft. Acquisition data points the same way, with 38 buys in Bastrop at a $196,540 median and 31 in Kyle at $188,700 versus Austin's $639,065.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Follow the exit map, not the old core

Wholesalers
Only 12 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds this edition versus 28 a year ago, and that count is a floor. The data suggests buyer demand has shifted toward Williamson, which took 31% of flip exits against 16% a year ago; Georgetown West (33 buys, $379,050 median), Round Rock (28 buys, $299,294) and Hutto (27 buys, $327,559) are where the active resellers are working. Low-basis exurban product also moved in volume: Bastrop 38 buys at $196,540, Elgin 30 at $205,332, Dale 27 at $185,069.

Underwrite the outer ring at $256/sqft, not $292

Flippers
Median flip resale came in at $453,297 and $256/sqft this edition, against $538,000 and $292/sqft a year ago, with the median exit 27.8 km from downtown versus 19.0 km. Worth checking whether your ARV comps still lean on 2025 in-town sales. Median hold ran 165 days versus 243 a year ago and 28% of flippers on record are net-negative lifetime, so the data argues for scoping to the shorter hold and pricing the resale at the outer-ring per-foot number from the start.

Basis is cheapest where flippers are not exiting

Landlords
Kyle bought at a $188,700 median ($150/sqft), Hutto at $327,559 ($154/sqft) and Liberty Hill at $412,965 ($147/sqft) — all well under Austin's $639,065 and $349/sqft. Bastrop (38 buys) and Dale (27 buys) recorded no flip exits at all this edition, meaning acquisitions there are being held, not turned. With 1,415 local landlords adding 2,823 units since the last drop, competition for those low-basis houses is real; the data suggests moving early in the $185,000–$215,000 band rather than chasing the Austin per-foot.

The gap is short-duration paper in Williamson and Hays

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 30 from 156 a year ago while the median ticket rose to $260,900 from $202,500, and seller-financed notes at 155 now outnumber private loans five to one. With flip holds at a median 165 days and exits concentrating in Georgetown, Round Rock and Kyle, the unfilled slot looks like 6-to-9-month paper in the $200,000–$450,000 range against outer-county single family. Note that measured loan-to-price ran at a 1.02 median over the trailing year, so verify the collateral basis independently rather than relying on the recorded price.

Small multi is priced below single family per foot

Multifamily investors
Investors bought 32 two-to-four unit properties at a $446,134 median and $214/sqft, under single family's $251/sqft and condo's $250/sqft. Small multi accounted for 5.6% of flip exits this edition versus 8.2% a year ago, so less of this stock is being bought to resell. Larger residential deals continue to clear separately — the five largest purchases were apartment assets in Leander, Round Rock, Austin, Kyle and Georgetown between $31.9M and $45.4M. Worth checking the 2-4 unit per-foot gap in the same outer zips where flip exits are now landing.

Flip Pipeline — Buying, Selling and Inventory

Homes bought by known flippers and homes they resold, per month, alongside the stock sitting in flipper hands. A house enters inventory when a flipper buys it and leaves on the next recorded sale — at any price, on any timeline, which is a wider net than the flip count elsewhere in this brief. A purchase stops counting after five years unsold, on the view that by then it is a hold rather than a flip in progress.

Solid = recorded, dashed = projected. Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
