August Edition — Land Share

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

472 of 1,086
Single-family purchases out of all 1,086 investor purchases this edition; 215 residential lots and 131 other-land parcels made up most of the balance

Only 472 of 1,086 investor purchases were standing single-family houses; lots and acreage took most of the rest

Single family accounted for 472 of 1,086 investor purchases this edition, at a $419,415 median and $251/sqft. Residential lots (215 at $302,469) and other land (131 at $478,789) took most of the balance, and the period's largest buyer, 528 W 162 LLC of New York, recorded 23 parcels of mostly Austin land for $133.1M. The resale side stayed almost entirely houses: 106 of 114 flips were single family. Builder new-build sales came in at 89 against 120 a year ago, so dirt is being accumulated faster than it is being converted into finished product.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

The buyer list this period is a land list

Wholesalers
Only 13 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds versus 34 a year ago, and that count is a floor. With 215 residential lots trading at a $302,469 median and 131 other-land parcels at $478,789, it is worth building a separate lot and acreage buyer list in Bastrop ($200,032 median, 48 buys), Elgin ($218,942, 34) and Dale ($185,069, 35) — three markets with heavy buying and almost no flip exits. Keep house contracts pointed at Kyle 78640 and Georgetown 78628, where eight flips each actually cleared.

Underwrite only where flips are visibly clearing

Flippers
114 flips sold at a $315,542 median, down from 187 a year ago, and 106 of them were single family at $322,529 and $140/sqft. The zips with real absorption were 78640 Kyle and 78628 Georgetown West (8 each), 78745 (6), and 78641, 78634 and 78665 (5 each). Zips with volume but no exits — 78616 Dale (35 buys, 0 flips) and 78610 Buda (27 buys, 0 flips) — are being bought for reasons other than resale. Hold assumptions of about 5.9 months, the measured median, look more realistic than a 90-day plan that only 19% of flips hit.

Check small multi and ring product on a per-foot basis

Landlords
Single family cleared at $419,415 and $251/sqft, condos at $297,150 and $250/sqft, and 2-4 unit product at $446,134 and $214/sqft — the cheapest per foot of the three. On geography, Jarrell at $119/sqft, Kyle at $155/sqft and Hutto at $158/sqft are where basis is lowest. Note that 1,415 local landlords added 2,823 units since the last drop and 2,457 first-time landlords appeared, so competition for the same ring rentals is not scarce; the data suggests leading with per-foot basis rather than headline price.

The gap is lot and land paper, not house bridges

Private lenders
36 private loans were recorded against 180 a year ago, while investors bought 215 residential lots at a $302,469 median and 131 other-land parcels at $478,789 — product banks generally will not finance. Seller carry is filling part of that hole, with 186 new notes at a $282,175 median, up in size from $200,000 a year ago. Loan sizes in the $150,000-$300,000 band match what the most active recorded lenders are actually writing, and note purchases at the current carry-back sizes are worth pricing against the same collateral.

Industrial is the cheapest per foot on the commercial board

Commercial investors
Retail/service traded 55 times at a $1.33M median and $427/sqft, office 6 times at $1.93M and $431/sqft, and industrial 12 times at $2.91M but only $201/sqft. Multi-family (unspecified) showed 13 deals at a $14.1M median. Bank and securitized holders remain visible on the buy side — Karnes County National Bank recorded 39 deals at a $3.07M median, which is also what distorts the 78752 Highland median. Note that 32 recorded values were excluded as implausible, so verify per-parcel allocations on portfolio transfers before comping.

Out-of-area money is buying a more expensive slice of the market than resident investors are. California (58) and New York (31) supplied the bulk of the out-of-state count.

36 — Private loans recorded, median $260,900

trending down · vs 180 a year ago
Recorded hard-money and private-note volume is a fraction of last year's, and the largest active lenders on record made only three to five loans apiece. Deals in the ring price bands are being funded off-record or with cash.

186 — New seller-financed notes held, median $282,175

trending down · vs 263 a year ago at a $200,000 median
Fewer carry-back deals, but each one is materially larger than a year ago. Seller paper remains the most visible substitute where recorded private lending has thinned.

$155/sqft — Median investor purchase price per square foot in Kyle (45 buys, $276,972 median)

trending down · vs $359/sqft in Austin ($720,000 median, 357 buys)
The per-foot spread between the core and the southern ring is more than two to one. Hutto ($158/sqft) and Jarrell ($119/sqft) sit in the same low-basis band.

89 — Builder new-build sales, median $458,850

trending down · vs 120 a year ago at $464,170
Vertical delivery is down about a quarter year over year while the same builders keep buying lots. Less new supply competing with resale product in the outer counties this period.

$6,500 — Median profit per flip across the lifetime record of 8,559 local flippers

holding steady · against 25,036 recorded flips, 28% of flippers net-negative
The long-run margin on this trade is thin and roughly one in four operators is underwater on cumulative flips. At a $315,542 clearing median this edition, rehab and carry assumptions carry the whole result.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
