August Edition — Pipeline Buildup

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

255 bought vs 55 resold
Flip acquisitions against flip resales this edition; a year ago it was 224 bought against 77 resold

Flippers are buying far faster than they are exiting: 255 acquisitions against 55 resales this edition, a net 200 homes into the pipeline versus 147 a year ago

Recorded deeds show flippers acquiring much faster than they exit. Flip purchases reached 255 this edition against 224 a year ago, while resales fell to 55 from 77 — a net 200 homes added to the pipeline versus 147 a year ago. The tracked pipeline stands at 3,887 homes, up from 3,856 in December. In the latest month, 88 held properties aged past the flip window while only 77 resold. Median hold on completed exits was 168 days, down from 260, so the buildup sits mostly in stock bought since March, when acquisitions hit 196.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Feed the zips where buying is heavy and exits are thin

Wholesalers
Only 12 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds versus 28 a year ago, and that count is a floor — but flipper acquisition demand is running 127.5 homes per month against 112 a year ago. The data suggests contract flow is worth concentrating where buys are dense and flip exits are rare: 78602 Bastrop (38 buys, 0 flips), 78621 Elgin (30 buys, 1 flip), 78616 Dale (27 buys, 0 flips) and 78640 Kyle (31 buys, 3 flips), all with estimated median buys roughly in the $185,000–$205,000 range.

Assume you are queuing behind 3,887 homes

Flippers
Acquisitions of 255 against 55 resales, with 88 properties aging past the flip window in the latest month versus 77 resold, point to a crowded exit rather than a scarce one. Worth stress-testing new buys against a 168-day median hold plus listing time, and against the fact that 93% of exit revenue ran through ten operators. This edition's exit product — three-bed, about 1,930 sqft, median 1996 build, 28.0 km out — is where the volume is; estimated gross margins near 26% exclude rehab and carry, which are not public record.

Shop the aging pipeline, not the active listings

Landlords
With 88 flips aging out in the latest month and a pipeline of 3,887, sellers holding past their intended window are the most likely source of negotiated buys. Single family made up 505 of this edition's investor purchases, condos 86, mobile/manufactured 30 and 2-4 units 32; all dollar figures here are estimates, so underwrite to your own rent evidence rather than to an estimated basis. Lower-basis flow clusters in 78621, 78640 and 78616; higher-basis flow in 78745 and 78641.

Two-thirds of investor purchases show no recorded purchase loan

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 30 from 156 a year ago, while trailing-12-month financed share sat at 32.8% and 212 loans were tied to rehab-funded purchases. The gap the data suggests is short-term acquisition and rehab paper on the 3,887-home pipeline, where hold times are running to a 168-day median. Note the repeat-borrower concentration in the recorded book: Kiavi Funding shows 6 loans to 3 borrowers, Lendz Financial 5 loans to 1, Nexus Series B 5 loans to 1. Rates and terms are not recorded and are not published here.

Small multi stays a thin slice of both sides of the market

Multifamily investors
2-4 unit product accounted for 32 of 882 investor purchases, and small multi made up 5.5% of flip resales versus 7.8% a year ago — one 2-4 unit resale in the whole edition. Seller-financed notes, a common structuring tool at this size, fell to 155 from 239 a year ago, so carry-back counterparties are scarcer. Estimated per-sqft figures on 2-4 units are below single family, but with 47% of prices back-computed these gaps are not measured facts; verify unit-level rents and recorded loan amounts before pricing.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
