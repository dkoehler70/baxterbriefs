August Edition — Exit Repricing

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$315,542 vs $359,100
Median flip resale price this edition versus a year ago, while the investor purchase median held at $408,018 against $405,583 a year ago

Flip exit prices absorbed the adjustment while acquisition pricing did not: resales cleared at $315,542 against a $408,018 investor purchase median

Flips sold at a median $315,542 this edition, down 12% from $359,100 a year ago, while the investor purchase median held at $408,018 against $405,583 a year ago. Volume moved the same way as price: 114 flips versus 187 a year ago and 160 in the prior period, a 39% year-over-year contraction that the buy side did not match at 1,086 purchases, down 14%. The pattern points to exit pricing carrying the adjustment while acquisition basis has not moved with it. Single-family accounted for 106 of the 114 flips, clearing at a median $322,529.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Pitch the ring to landlords, not to flippers underwriting last year's exit

Wholesalers
Only 13 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds this edition versus 34 a year ago, and that count is a floor. The buyer depth sits in Bastrop 78602 (48 buys, $200,032 median), Kyle 78640 (46), Dale 78616 (35, $185,069) and Elgin 78621 (34, $218,942), where flip counts are low but buy counts are not. The data suggests contracts priced to a hold, not to a $315,542 flip exit, are the ones with a live buyer list.

Underwrite to a $315,542 exit and a six-month carry

Flippers
The median flip resale came in at $315,542, down from $359,100 a year ago, with single-family at $322,529 and $140/sqft. The latest measurable hold period ran a median 5.9 months, middle half 3.6 to 9.0 months, and 13% of flips took over a year. Worth checking whether your current spreads survive last year's exit assumption minus 12%, with carry sized to the long tail rather than the median.

Basis is where the advantage is; the metro median has not moved

Landlords
Investor purchases held at a $408,018 median versus $405,583 a year ago, so the buy side offers no discount at the metro level. The gaps are by geography and product: Kyle at $155/sqft, Hutto at $158/sqft, Elgin at $165/sqft against Austin's $359/sqft. Condos (86 purchases, $297,150 median) and 2-4 units (32 at $446,134) are the two product lines where recorded entry prices sit below single family's $419,415.

The gap to fill is the repriced flip exit, not the acquisition

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 36 from 180 a year ago, and seller-financed notes to 186 from 263. With flip resale medians at $315,542 against $359,100 a year ago, the data suggests sizing bridge paper to today's exit rather than trailing comps, and extending term past the 5.9-month median hold given 13% of flips ran over a year. The active names on the record are small: the top private lender booked 5 loans.

Land and small commercial carried the dollars while housing volume fell

Commercial investors
Investors recorded 215 residential lot purchases at a $302,469 median and 131 other-land purchases at $478,789, against 472 single-family buys. Retail/service logged 55 deals at a $1.33M median ($427/sqft), industrial 12 at $2.91M. Concentration is real: one New York buyer took 23 parcels for $133.1M, mostly Austin land, and a builder recorded a $95.1M Manchaca purchase, so top-line dollar totals should be read net of a few names.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Austin-Round Rock-Georgetown MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
