August Edition — Margin Squeeze

Analyzing transactions recorded May 1 – June 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

$81,000 vs $95,000
Median gross margin on flips resold this edition, against the same two-month window a year ago

Median gross margin per flip fell to $81,000 from $95,000 a year ago, and exits are clearing at 1.26 times assessed value versus 1.33 — the squeeze is on the resale, not on hold time

The 122 flips resold this edition carried a median gross margin of $81,000, down from $95,000 across 211 resales a year ago, and margin as a share of basis fell to 40.4% from 47.8%. Exits cleared at 1.26 times county assessed value against 1.33 a year ago, and resale price per square foot slipped to $203.30 from $208.99. Hold time did not lengthen — the median was 177.5 days versus 190 — so the compression sits in the exit price, not in operator patience. Rehab and carry are not in the public record; these are gross figures, and the squeeze on net is larger.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Price assignments to a 40% gross spread, not last year's 48%

Wholesalers
Only 5 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds this period, at a $20,000 median spread against $24,990 in the prior period — and that count is a floor. With flip margin percentage at 40.4% versus 47.8% a year ago, contracts priced off last year's ARV assumptions are the ones that fall out of escrow. The data suggests testing the zips gaining flip share — 85706 (9.8% of resales, from 4.7%) and 85641 Vail (4.9%, from 0.5%) — and leading with 1970s stock, which drew 211 investor purchases this period, the largest decade bucket.

Underwrite exits at $203/sqft and 1.26x assessed, not 2025 comps

Flippers
Flips resold this edition ran a median $284,750 on 1,456 sqft, built around 1977, three beds, about 14 km from downtown — the same product as a year ago at a lower clearing price. Worth checking every deal against the two benchmarks that moved: $203.30/sqft resale (from $208.99) and 1.26x county assessed value (from 1.33). Hold time is not the problem at 177.5 days versus 190, so extra rehab scope buys less than trimming basis does.

Condo and manufactured basis is where the per-foot gap is widest

Landlords
Investors paid a $240,000 median for 209 condos at $204/sqft and $118,147 for 97 mobile or manufactured homes at $117/sqft, against $339,950 and $204/sqft for 804 single-family purchases. With 597 local landlords adding units (+735 units) since the last drop and 1,290 first-time local landlords appearing, competition for the standard three-bed house is broad; the cheaper product types are where the same rent dollar buys more square feet. Lower-median-buy zips this period include 85705 at $195,000 and 85713 at $220,000.

Rehab dollars are the gap as recorded private loans go to 13

Private lenders
Across the trailing twelve months, 645 of 822 observed investor purchases carried financing (78.5%), at a $205,742 median loan and 0.99 loan-to-price, with rehab funds on 41.2% of them (266 loans). Recorded private loans, meanwhile, ran 13 this edition against 208 a year ago at a $122,000 median. The active lender file is concentrated — FlipFast Loans at 48 loans across 6 borrowers, ArchWest at 23 across 4 — which suggests room for lenders willing to fund rehab for operators outside those repeat relationships. Note rates and terms are not recorded and are not published here.

Small multifamily stayed out of the flip channel entirely

Multifamily investors
Not one of the 122 flips resold this period was small multifamily (0.0% share, against 0.5% a year ago), so 2-4 unit stock is trading as hold product: 28 purchases at a $450,000 median and $275/sqft. Apartment 5+ recorded 9 purchases at a $1,245,000 median and $162.64/sqft — the lowest per-foot basis of any residential product with meaningful volume. Worth checking whether the per-foot discount at 5+ units survives your own unit-count and condition review before treating it as a basis advantage.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Tucson-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated May 1 – June 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
