August Edition — Thinning Exits

Analyzing transactions recorded March 1 – April 30, 2026

This Issue August 2026 edition

462 vs 682
Operators recording at least one flip resale this edition, against the same period a year ago

Flip exits are thinning faster than investor buying: 462 operators sold 1,047 homes this edition against 682 selling 1,441 a year ago, while investor purchases held flat

Resale supply from flippers is contracting on both counts: 1,047 flips sold this edition versus 1,441 a year ago, produced by 462 operators versus 682. Investor purchase volume did not follow — 3,732 recorded buys against 3,724 a year ago — so the pullback is concentrated in who is finishing and listing projects, not in who is acquiring. Pricing on the exits barely moved, median $328,000 against $336,500, and gross margin held at $68,000 versus $67,900. Fewer sellers competing on the same rehab product is the operating condition this period.

Investor Playbook

What this issue's numbers suggest checking, by investor type. Observations from recorded data — not investment, legal, or tax advice.

Sell into the zips where operators are still closing

Wholesalers
Only 4 assignments surfaced on recorded deeds against 86 a year ago, and that count is a floor. With 462 buyers active instead of 682, the data suggests tightening lists to zips with the most flip resales this edition — 30016 and 30127 at 23 each, 30032 at 20, 30052 at 16 — and to the flip buy box the records show: about 1,728 sqft, 3 bed, median year built 1986.

Less exit competition, unchanged pricing math

Flippers
Median resale held at $180/sqft against $181 a year ago, gross margin at $68,000 (31.5%), hold time 180 days versus 184. Cobb took 15.1% of resales this edition against 12.3%, while Fulton slipped to 13.7% from 15.8%; worth checking whether your acquisition basis in Cobb still supports the $328,000 median exit before adding there.

Basis gap between product types is the decision this period

Landlords
Single family bought at a $310,000 median and $167/sqft; condos at $280,000 and $242/sqft; mobile/manufactured at $102,000 and $87/sqft. With the overall investor median down to $307,750 from $325,000, the data suggests pricing offers off $/sqft rather than headline price, especially in Covington ($195,000 median buy) and Decatur ($215,000).

The small rehab loan is the visible hole

Private lenders
Recorded private loans fell to 28 from 54; over the trailing 12 months 64.6% of observed purchases were financed, 29.8% of those financed loans funded rehab (865), median loan $197,631 at 0.83 loan-to-price. Kiavi wrote 80 loans with a 73.8% rehab share and a $191,250 median purchase. Worth checking demand below that median loan size, where recorded private paper has thinned most.

Small multi is a thin but repricing tape

Multifamily investors
Eighty-nine 2-4 unit purchases at a $660,000 median ($277/sqft) and 29 apartment 5+ purchases at a $1.71M median ($116/sqft) frame the two ends. Small multi was 1.3% of flip resales this edition against 0.1% a year ago — a small base, but the first sign operators are exiting 2-4 unit product; worth tracking before assuming a thin resale bid.

Methodology

Source Data

County recorder & assessor via FARES
Every figure is computed from recorded instruments (deeds, mortgages, assignments) and assessor property records for the five counties of the Atlanta-Elyria MSA. No surveys, no estimates, no listings data.
This edition analyzes transactions dated March 1 – April 30, 2026, as recorded through the 2026-08 data extract.

Who Counts as an Investor

Transaction-pattern identification
Investors are entities whose recorded transaction history looks like investing: rental holding (landlords), short-hold resales (flippers), private mortgage lending, note holding, or contract assignment (wholesalers). Banks, homebuilders, iBuyers, SFR funds, and government entities are identified by name and reported separately from small investors.

How Comparisons Work

Attribution deltas + same-window comparisons
The Market Pulse reports activity attributed between successive data drops, from the per-investor lifetime totals maintained upstream — periods differ in length, so per-month rates are shown. Elsewhere, "vs. year ago" compares the same calendar months one year earlier, counted from the same data extract. Transfers under $5,000 (quitclaims, partial interests) are excluded everywhere. Counts lag recording by 4–8 weeks, and the newest month can grow up to ~15% as late records arrive — direction is reliable before magnitude.

Known Limits

Floors, not ceilings
Wholesale assignments only appear when they touch a recorded deed, so those counts are a floor. Flip hold times are measured from the prior recorded sale. Names are the owner of record, which may be an LLC or trust.
